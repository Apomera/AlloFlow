# Friendship Workshop: starting with an invitation and a choice

The Starting tab now teaches the exchange around an opening line: checking whether the moment works, making an optional invitation, responding to what happens next, and stepping back. The previous carousel described several scripts as producing connection or warmth and gave little guidance for declines or uncertainty. Those guarantees and unsupported research shorthand in the contextual help card are replaced with conditional, practical guidance.

## Learning design

All 18 contexts remain represented in the same order across elementary, middle and high school. Every context includes a timing, access or permission check; an adaptable opening; a qualified explanation; and a possible follow-up if conversation is welcomed. The examples no longer assume someone alone is lonely, someone with different experiences should explain an identity, a knowledgeable classmate should tutor, or an old friend necessarily wants to reconnect.

Three explicitly fictional response branches support rehearsal: welcome, decline/request for space, and uncertainty. A welcome applies to that exchange and can change. A decline does not require bargaining or an explanation. An unclear response leaves room for processing time and different communication methods without treating uncertainty as permission for repeated contact. The activity does not infer a real person's intentions from body language or offer a guaranteed path to friendship.

Four optional notes address the moment and communication access, opening words or actions, responding or stepping back, and what to notice or adjust. Learners can explore multiple responses without overwriting notes. A separate own-example context supports transfer. The preview labels the explored response as fictional and the practice as a draft, not a prediction. Reading, thinking and practising with a trusted person are valid alternatives to writing; a yes from another person is not the measure of success.

## Pedagogical source and limits

[CASEL's SEL framework](https://casel.org/what-is-sel/), reviewed during this SEL enhancement series on September 19, 2026, identifies communication, supportive relationships, seeking help and considering consequences within context as relevant skills. Those broad principles inform this authored rehearsal structure. The scripts and response examples are teaching materials, not a validated social assessment or a method for classifying another person's intent.

## Persistence and accessibility

`starterSelections[band]` stores context and `starterDrafts[band:context]` stores the optional notes and explored response. The original `starterIdx` still chooses the initial context when no valid saved selection exists; its historical value is not rewritten. Existing journal, coach, repair, endings and digital-activity records remain intact. Malformed new containers and field values recover safely, and unknown draft properties are retained. This activity makes no provider request, sends no message and awards no completion points.

Native labeled selects, disclosures and textareas support keyboard use, with a polite announcement of the selected fictional response. Controls use 44px targets and 16px input text. Light, dark and high-contrast palettes preserve the established theme contract. Canonical and public files are kept byte-identical. Focused tests cover all contexts and three response branches, saved-note isolation, legacy positions, malformed data, keyboard use, three 320px themes and actual-hub return/reopen. Validation evidence is recorded in `reports/sel-friendship-starting`. No push, deployment or packaged build is included.

Final validation: all 66 selected checks passed on their first run. All 72 SEL tools rendered, three scoped axe scans found no violations and nine phone layouts were reviewed. Source/public parity, syntax and scoped whitespace passed. Detailed counts and normalized logs are in `reports/sel-friendship-starting`. No push, deployment or packaged build.
