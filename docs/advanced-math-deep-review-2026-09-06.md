# Advanced math: deep UI, UX, and pedagogy pass

Reviewed all five tools listed in the app's Advanced Math category: Function Grapher, Inequality Explorer, Calculus, Algebra CAS, and Graphing Calculator. The pass includes their main modes, practice and challenge views, guided activities, and all twelve calculus visualizations. Source and public copies are synchronized.

## Improvements implemented

| Tool | Finding | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Algebra CAS | Unary minus had the wrong precedence relative to powers. Balance steps depended on an AI response and could replay the whole operation history against an already transformed equation. | `-x^2` now means `-(x^2)`. Linear balance operations run locally, apply once, and preserve exact rational coefficients. Dividing by three and multiplying back keeps the equation exact. |
| Algebra CAS | Zero multiplication could destroy information, and completed steps disappeared when the equation was solved. | Zero multiplication/division are rejected with an explanation. Identity and contradiction messages distinguish all-real-number solutions from no solution. Worked steps remain visible after solving; editing or resetting clears stale feedback. Learners can add/subtract x-terms. The beam and pans now fit phone canvases, labels remain visible, and the diagram redraws when resized. |
| Function Grapher | A central difference could suggest a horizontal tangent at an absolute-value corner. Undefined trace values disappeared. | Supported families use analytic derivatives. Corners and domain boundaries get explicit feedback; invalid tangent lines are suppressed. Trace text wraps on phones. |
| Function Grapher | Integrals could silently discard undefined or overflowing samples. Teaching copy said roots cross the axis and tangents meet a curve exactly once. | Domain and numerical limits are explained, and invalid area estimates/shading are withheld. Root and tangent explanations now include touching roots, corners, and possible later intersections. |
| Inequality Explorer | Partial regex matches could graph a different inequality from the one entered. Reversed compound bounds could produce a negative-width band. | Only complete supported forms are accepted. Unsupported forms get guidance. Impossible compound intervals show the empty set, without an invalid band. A strict inequality explicitly excludes its boundary. |
| Calculus | Signed integral and total geometric area were easy to confuse. One animation called a numerical reference “exact.” | A linked area breakdown splits the quadratic at its real roots, distinguishes above/below magnitudes, shows their proportions, and connects subtraction to the net integral. It waits until an estimate is submitted. Numerical references are labeled approximate. |
| Graphing Calculator | Undefined table entries used cryptic placeholders, and unavailable traces could resemble an empty function list. A symmetric difference could draw a false tangent at a corner. | Table and trace feedback name undefined real values or evaluation errors. Tangent estimates compare one-sided slopes and include a readable estimate or explanation of why one is unavailable. Table coaching explains rounding. Quadratic inquiry now has its own linked graph, a vertex marker, a fixed-window explanation, and correct constant-function feedback when a = 0. |
| Theme and language refinements | Several unstyled fields inherited dark browser backgrounds with dark text. High-contrast mode conflicted with hard-coded pale feedback backgrounds and local text overrides. | Inputs and reflection fields pair foreground/background colors. Inquiry feedback and calculus colors respect high-contrast mode. The expanded reflection checkbox meets target-size requirements, and estimate controls stack at phone widths. Developer-facing classification jargon and an unsupported AP coverage statistic were removed from learner-facing copy. |

The balance model uses a small, bounded rational parser for linear expressions in **x**, including decimals, fractions, parentheses, addition/subtraction of linear terms, and multiplication/division by nonzero constants. Nonlinear expressions are directed to the existing Solve mode. It rejects malformed decimal tokens and unsupported variables rather than silently treating another letter as x. Other CAS modes retain their existing AI workflows.

## Further opportunities

1. **Organize long guided activities into shorter stages.** Several inquiry and discovery views combine controls, logs, reflections, and explanations on one long page. A deliberate sequence with a persistent current question would reduce scrolling while preserving evidence and keyboard focus.
2. **Strengthen numerical-analysis provenance.** Arbitrary-function roots, intersections, and tangent estimates are numerical evidence. Root scans can miss touching roots or roots outside the window; the tangent agreement check is a heuristic, not a proof of differentiability. A future analysis panel could show interval, tolerance, and a substitution check together.
3. **Extend domain reasoning.** Function Grapher currently explains when its finite-interval overlay cannot evaluate an improper integral. A separate improper-integral activity could compare truncated intervals and limits without suggesting that every undefined endpoint causes divergence.
4. **Connect balance steps to manipulatives.** Exact equations and persistent steps now supply a reliable base for a linked tile model. The beam represents equality for a solution; a future substitution view could show why a trial x does or does not satisfy the equation.
5. **Test AI-generated teaching paths with provider fixtures.** Solve explanations, tutoring, and generated practice deserve dedicated checks for contradictory steps, unsupported answers, retries, and unavailable providers. This pass reviews their rendered interfaces; it does not claim to verify every generated explanation.

No broad build, staging, commit, or deployment was performed. Unrelated shared-workspace edits were preserved.
