# Decision Workshop: conditional consequence reasoning

Updated 2026-09-12.

Open Decision Workshop and choose **Consequence Map**. It is the starting tab when no saved tab is selected. All 30 existing consequence scenarios have been revised, with ten per grade band. The activity now distinguishes possible effects from predictions, asks what an outcome depends on, and supports changing a plan when new information matters.

## Learning sequence

1. Read a fictional case and identify people whose needs or effort may be affected. An optional model explains a possible benefit or intended effect, a possible cost, and what is still unknown. These are not equally likely predictions, and potential benefits do not cancel safety, rights or consent.
2. Explore a possible near-term effect, another plausible path, and what might happen if a pattern continued. There is no required five-year prediction. State a condition or reason for each possibility.
3. Consider who benefits, who carries the costs, whose perspective is missing, and what information, permission or support is needed. Do not infer people's feelings from the scene alone.
4. Identify an adjustment within the learner's control and a review point. Compare a case-specific model of a smaller trial, changed route, suitable support, or practical check. Some actions cannot be undone, and immediate safety should not wait for a completed worksheet.
5. Optionally keep one earlier version of the current notes. Then explore a hypothetical changed condition and explain what to keep or revise. A favorable or unfavorable outcome alone cannot establish whether the earlier reasoning was sound.

This design is informed by [CASEL's responsible decision-making framework](https://casel.org/what-is-sel/), including consideration of safety, benefits and consequences for oneself and others. The framework informs the learning aims; it is not evidence of the effectiveness of this implementation.

## Nuance and learner choice

The revised cases avoid treating disclosure, constant practice, staying on a team, or sacrificing income as universally better. They distinguish invitations from pressure, help-seeking from gossip, safety support from investigation, and a personal boundary from a character flaw. Course selection, paid work, volunteering and time before college depend on actual conditions and supports. AI assistance in an application depends on the specific rules, rather than a universal claim that all assistance is allowed or prohibited.

For brief practice, compare one possible effect with one condition it depends on. For deeper work, compare who bears a cost, identify an alternative route, preserve the reasoning and revisit it after the hypothetical change. Think, discuss, draw, sign or use AAC instead of typing. Personal disclosure, writing and saving a version are optional. No XP, score, completed-map claim or AI response is generated by this workflow.

## State and legacy notes

Current drafts use `mapDrafts`, keyed by stable scenario ID; `mapSelected` retains the selected case separately per grade band. Seven reasoning fields, a changed-condition reveal, and a separate revision note stay in ordinary tool/project state. One optional `snapshot` copies the seven current fields and stays unchanged when those fields are edited. It is an earlier version of writing, not evidence that the proposed action happened. Keeping it does not write a project file; use the Hub save/export controls to keep a project copy.

The earlier short-, medium- and long-term flat fields lacked a reliable case/grade-band link. They remain visible as an unassigned map. The explicit copy action fills only blank current fields, preserves existing writing, and retains the old copy. It does not manufacture a scenario link or clear older notes. Historical counts and badges remain stored; this activity does not increase them. The former required Save Map/Next Scenario sequence is replaced by a case picker that keeps other drafts.

Invalid selected IDs and indices fall back to a valid case. Non-string note values display as blank; invalid snapshot array values are not used as a version. Case selection and reveal/version buttons retain keyboard focus. The map uses native disclosures, explicit labels, 16 px inputs, 44 px controls and wrapping layouts.

## Case coverage

### Elementary

- **Explaining unfinished homework:** What if several students misunderstood the instructions?
- **Supporting someone at the bus stop:** What if the targeting continued after an interruption?
- **A sustainable lunch plan:** What if the classmate did not want their situation discussed with other students?
- **A place at the table:** What if they preferred another table after the correction?
- **Repair after an accident:** What if the friend wanted space and did not want you to fix it?
- **Getting help with the work:** What if the example used a format you could not access?
- **Inviting without pressure:** What if they said they wanted quiet today but might join tomorrow?
- **Taking space after disagreement:** What if the friend kept messaging after a clear request for space?
- **A practice routine that can change:** What if the practice time stopped fitting the household schedule?
- **Deciding when to ask for help:** What if there were a safety concern rather than a minor classroom difference?

### Middle

- **Raising a concern publicly:** What if a private request had already failed and several students described a pattern?
- **Discussing a difficult grade:** What if the grade reflected inaccessible instructions rather than lack of study?
- **Asking to change a school rule:** What if some students needed devices for communication or other access support?
- **Responding to a friend who is struggling:** What if the friend did not want to talk to you but wanted help finding an adult?
- **Support after a presentation:** What if the mocking continued the next day?
- **Leaving a team:** What if an injury, inaccessible practice, or unsafe treatment were involved?
- **Sending an apology:** What if the friend had already asked for no contact?
- **Choosing a course that fits:** What if a support change made the current course accessible without increasing total workload?
- **Witnessing hallway targeting:** What if the incident was still happening while others were filming?
- **A sustainable volunteer commitment:** What if transport became unavailable for part of the month?

### High

- **AI assistance and authorship:** What if brainstorming was allowed but generated personal statements were not?
- **Documenting an unfair pattern:** What if students supported the concern but did not consent to being named?
- **Ending a difficult friendship:** What if the person kept contacting you after being asked to stop?
- **Comparing paid work and an internship:** What if the internship gained reliable funding or the paid job offered mentoring?
- **Planning a year before college:** What if a funding offer could not be deferred?
- **Seeking help about a safety concern:** What if someone appeared to need urgent help right now?
- **Choosing what to share in an essay:** What if a family member did not consent to identifiable details?
- **Requesting a fair grading review:** What if an earlier request was dismissed or you feared retaliation?
- **Changing social-media use:** What if one app was your main accessible connection to a community?
- **Supporting a sibling without taking their blame:** What if either sibling feared being hurt rather than receiving a routine consequence?

## Validation and limits

Validation passed **527 existing regression checks and 38 browser workflows** (36 focused workflows and two actual-hub checks), with two existing regression skips. All 72 tools passed initial-render smoke checks. Three full-rule axe scans scoped to the activity returned zero violations; dark effects and high-contrast revision phone captures were visually reviewed. Syntax, source/public byte parity and scoped whitespace checks passed. Final results are archived in `reports/sel-decision-depth/validation.json`. The focused browser tests use the actual tool renderer with simulated callbacks. They cover the model, adjustment and changed condition for every case; optional writing; immutable earlier versions; independent case and grade-band state; serialized restoration; non-overwriting legacy recovery; keyboard focus; and phone layouts in three themes. Actual hub return/reopen is checked separately.

Other Decision Workshop tabs, the older Decision Tree, AI advice, and the printable generic decision template are outside this pass. These checks do not establish a real project-file round trip, packaged Desktop behavior, live assistive-technology usability, classroom outcomes or clinical effectiveness. Educators should review language, culture, access and local support routes before use. Nothing was deployed.
