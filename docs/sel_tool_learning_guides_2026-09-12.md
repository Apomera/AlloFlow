# SEL tool learning guides

Updated 2026-09-12. This reference contains the same authored guide content embedded in the Hub. Open a tool and expand **Learning guide and ways to practice** to use its guide.

All 72 registered tools are covered. These are optional instructional scaffolds, not a certification of each tool’s internal content, effectiveness, accessibility, or clinical claims. Existing age guidance, content notices and safety routes still apply. Guide use does not record completion or submit learner data.

Design sources: [CASEL explicit SEL instruction](https://schoolguide.casel.org/focus-area-3/classroom/explicit-sel-instruction/) and [CAST choice and autonomy](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/interests-identities/choice-autonomy/). The sources inform purposeful practice and learner choice; they do not evaluate this implementation.

This dated reference is a review snapshot; update it when changing the authored guide content.

For authors: edit `sel_hub/sel_learning_guides.json`, then run `node dev-tools/build_sel_learning_guides.cjs` to embed the content in the source/public hub. `--check` verifies registry coverage, required fields and synchronized embeds. The browser does not fetch the JSON separately.

## Self-Advocacy Workshop

Tool ID: `advocacy`.

**What you can explore**

Prepare a clear request for something that helps you participate.

**A worked example**

A learner says, "An example would help me begin. Could you show one?" The request names a need and a useful change.

**Try one step**

Use a fictional task. Name its barrier, one requested support, and someone who could help.

**Look more closely**

What makes the request specific? What if the first person cannot help?

**A possible next use**

Rehearse a shorter version using words, a drawing, or AAC.

**Scope and limits**

A clear request does not guarantee agreement. Responsibility for access does not rest only with the learner.

## Anxiety Toolkit

Tool ID: `anxietyToolkit`.

**What you can explore**

Separate a worry from a practical question or support you can act on.

**A worked example**

A character worries about a new classroom. They cannot know the whole day in advance, but can ask for a map and meeting point.

**Try one step**

Choose a fictional worry. Identify one fact, one uncertainty, and one possible support.

**Look more closely**

Which part needs information, and which cannot be settled right now?

**A possible next use**

Choose a person or resource to consult about a similar uncertainty.

**Scope and limits**

This is educational practice, not diagnosis or a requirement to confront feared situations alone.

## Behavioral Activation

Tool ID: `behavioralActivation`.

**What you can explore**

Plan a manageable activity and notice whether its size and supports fit.

**A worked example**

A character puts paper nearby and tries one small sketch instead of planning a finished picture.

**Try one step**

Plan a brief activity with a first action, available materials, and an easier version.

**Look more closely**

What helped you begin, regardless of whether your mood changed?

**A possible next use**

Keep, shrink, replace, or pause the activity based on what you noticed.

**Scope and limits**

Completion and ratings do not measure recovery. This planner is not a course of therapy.

## Big Feelings (Anger)

Tool ID: `bigFeelings`.

**What you can explore**

Distinguish feeling angry from deciding what to do next.

**A worked example**

A character feels angry about interruptions. They pause the discussion and ask for help protecting their work time.

**Try one step**

Using a fictional interruption, name a possible need and a response that protects people and belongings.

**Look more closely**

What could change in the setting, as well as in the response?

**A possible next use**

Practice one request for space or support that fits another setting.

**Scope and limits**

Anger is not a character flaw. Practice does not excuse harm or require negotiating when unsafe.

## Body Story

Tool ID: `bodyStory`.

**What you can explore**

Examine body-related messages through comfort, access, and respect.

**A worked example**

An advertisement suggests only one body type belongs in a sport. A character asks whose bodies and participation options are missing.

**Try one step**

Choose a fictional media message. Separate what it shows from what it asks viewers to believe.

**Look more closely**

How could the message include more bodies without ranking them?

**A possible next use**

Identify a change to a message, space, or activity that would improve inclusion.

**Scope and limits**

No measurements, appearance judgments, food records, or personal body disclosure are needed.

## Care Constellations

Tool ID: `careConstellations`.

**What you can explore**

Explore care as something people give, receive, and arrange together.

**A worked example**

A character gets a lift from a neighbor and helps a sibling find a book. These are forms of care, not debts to balance.

**Try one step**

Map two fictional care connections. Describe the help and whether it is wanted and available.

**Look more closely**

Is anyone expected to give more than they can?

**A possible next use**

Practice asking about someone's capacity before offering or requesting help.

**Scope and limits**

Care is not a score or an obligation to stay in a harmful relationship. Roles can replace names.

## Career Compass

Tool ID: `careerCompass`.

**What you can explore**

Use interests to generate career questions and possible experiences.

**A worked example**

A character enjoys organizing an event. They explore several planning roles rather than treating an interest code as a job assignment.

**Try one step**

Compare two roles connected to an interest. List a question about each role's work, preparation, or accessibility.

**Look more closely**

What experience would test your assumptions?

**A possible next use**

Prepare a question for an interview or find a first-person account of the work.

**Scope and limits**

A self-check does not determine ability or future opportunity. Career information needs current local verification.

## Circles of Support

Tool ID: `circlesOfSupport`.

**What you can explore**

Describe support without ranking your worth by your network.

**A worked example**

A character lists a friend for shared interests and a staff member for arranging access. Each offers a different kind of support.

**Try one step**

Create a fictional map with two people or roles and describe their possible support.

**Look more closely**

Does closeness match availability? Who could help arrange missing support?

**A possible next use**

Choose a practical question about reaching a support person.

**Scope and limits**

There is no ideal number of people in a circle. Paid support and chosen relationships can both matter.

## Civic Action & Hope

Tool ID: `civicAction`.

**What you can explore**

Connect an issue with a feasible, informed action.

**A worked example**

A club notices an inaccessible event notice. It asks affected students what format would work and proposes a revision.

**Try one step**

Choose a school-level issue. Identify who is affected, whose input is missing, and one proportionate action.

**Look more closely**

Would the action address the barrier? Who should help decide?

**A possible next use**

Plan how to seek support and check whether the change helped.

**Scope and limits**

Public advocacy and personal disclosure are optional. A learner is not responsible for solving structural injustice alone.

## Community & Culture

Tool ID: `community`.

**What you can explore**

Explore belonging through curiosity about experiences and shared spaces.

**A worked example**

A celebration assumes everyone follows one tradition. A character asks what choices would help people participate.

**Try one step**

Use a fictional event. Identify an assumption and a question that invites varied experiences.

**Look more closely**

Does the question avoid making one person speak for a whole group?

**A possible next use**

Try an inclusive invitation or access question in a future group plan.

**Scope and limits**

Cultural labels do not predict an individual's beliefs, needs, or behavior.

## Self-Compassion

Tool ID: `compassion`.

**What you can explore**

Respond to a mistake with accuracy, care, and a useful next step.

**A worked example**

Instead of "I ruin everything," a character tries "I missed this step; I can ask what to repair and what help I need."

**Try one step**

Rewrite a fictional harsh judgment into a specific description plus a supportive action.

**Look more closely**

Does the wording acknowledge the problem without attacking the person?

**A possible next use**

Keep a phrase you could offer after a small mistake.

**Scope and limits**

Compassion does not require denying harm, feeling positive, or forgiving another person.

## Conflict Resolution Lab

Tool ID: `conflict`.

**What you can explore**

Compare safe ways to handle a low-stakes disagreement.

**A worked example**

Two characters want the quiet work area. They describe their needs and consider turns, another location, or asking for help.

**Try one step**

Rehearse one neutral description, one listening question, and two possible arrangements.

**Look more closely**

Which needs does each option meet? Is anyone pressured to agree?

**A possible next use**

Choose a phrase for checking agreement and revisiting an arrangement.

**Scope and limits**

Use fictional, low-stakes disagreements. Bullying, coercion, or danger call for support, not equal-responsibility negotiation.

## Conflict Theater (Beta)

Tool ID: `conflicttheater`.

**What you can explore**

Practice mediation questions in a fictional disagreement.

**A worked example**

A mediator asks, "What would each of you want understood before suggesting a solution?"

**Try one step**

Choose a fictional scene. Separate each character's statement from your assumptions before replying.

**Look more closely**

Which question made space for different perspectives? What remained unresolved?

**A possible next use**

Rehearse one clarification question for an everyday disagreement.

**Scope and limits**

AI characters are simulations, not interpretations of real people. Do not use this activity to mediate abuse or danger.

## Coping Toolkit

Tool ID: `coping`.

**What you can explore**

Compare comfortable supports and notice their fit.

**A worked example**

Breath focus feels uncomfortable to a character. They choose to look at a still object or ask for a quieter place instead.

**Try one step**

Read one strategy before deciding whether to try, adapt, observe, or skip it.

**Look more closely**

Did it feel helpful, unchanged, or uncomfortable? What setting or support mattered?

**A possible next use**

Choose an alternative and a way to stop or ask for help.

**Scope and limits**

No strategy works for everyone. Stop uncomfortable practice; calm is not a completion requirement.

## Cost-Benefit Grid

Tool ID: `costBenefit`.

**What you can explore**

Compare choices across time and from more than one viewpoint.

**A worked example**

Joining a club could offer connection, but takes time a character needs for another responsibility.

**Try one step**

Name short-term and longer-term benefits and costs of a fictional choice, including effects on others.

**Look more closely**

Which predictions are uncertain? What information could change the comparison?

**A possible next use**

Identify a question to answer before deciding and when to review the choice.

**Scope and limits**

Counting items in boxes does not determine the right decision. Consent and safety cannot always be traded against convenience.

## Crew Protocols

Tool ID: `crewProtocols`.

**What you can explore**

Choose a group structure that fits a purpose and preserves participation choices.

**A worked example**

A facilitator models passing and offers drawing or listening before inviting spoken contributions.

**Try one step**

Plan a protocol's purpose, instructions, response choices, and ways to pause or pass.

**Look more closely**

Who might be excluded by timing, language, or format?

**A possible next use**

Try one access adjustment and ask participants whether it helped.

**Scope and limits**

A group protocol is not permission to demand disclosure or make peers responsible for handling a crisis.

## Crisis Companion

Tool ID: `crisiscompanion`.

**What you can explore**

Locate support and understand the limits of a peer-support role.

**A worked example**

A practice phrase is, "You matter to me. Let's get someone who can help us with this."

**Try one step**

For learning, locate the tool's help options and identify a trusted adult or service you could contact.

**Look more closely**

What would help you reach support? What is outside a friend's responsibility?

**A possible next use**

Keep a help-seeking phrase or identify where support information is available.

**Scope and limits**

If support is needed now, use immediate-help options rather than finishing this guide. This activity is not emergency monitoring.

## Culture Explorer

Tool ID: `cultureExplorer`.

**What you can explore**

Ask specific cultural questions and evaluate how answers are represented.

**A worked example**

A learner asks about a community's celebration in a named place and time, then seeks an account from that community.

**Try one step**

Choose a focused question. Identify whose voice an answer represents and what needs checking.

**Look more closely**

Could it flatten differences within a community or confuse past and present?

**A possible next use**

Check one claim using a community-authored source before sharing it.

**Scope and limits**

AI descriptions, images, and audio can be inaccurate or stereotyped. No generated example represents an entire culture.

## DEAR MAN

Tool ID: `dearMan`.

**What you can explore**

Build an assertive request while considering choice and safety.

**A worked example**

A character says, "The instructions moved quickly. Could I have them in writing so I can begin?"

**Try one step**

Use a fictional ask. Separate the observable situation, your need, and a specific request.

**Look more closely**

Which parts make the request understandable? What could remain flexible?

**A possible next use**

Rehearse a short version and backup request in words, writing, or AAC.

**Scope and limits**

The script does not require eye contact, a particular voice, or negotiating away a boundary. It cannot guarantee agreement.

## Decision Workshop

Tool ID: `decisions`.

**What you can explore**

Compare options, likely effects, and missing information before acting.

**A worked example**

A character finds an uncredited image and considers seeking permission, finding a licensed image, or making their own.

**Try one step**

Name two options for a fictional decision, who they affect, and one uncertainty.

**Look more closely**

Which consequence is likely, which is only possible, and what could you check?

**A possible next use**

Plan a pause question for a similar choice.

**Scope and limits**

An activity score or AI suggestion is not proof that a choice is ethical in every context.

## Digital Wellbeing Studio

Tool ID: `digitalWellbeing`.

**What you can explore**

Consider whether a digital habit serves your needs and boundaries.

**A worked example**

A character values friend messages but finds notifications interrupting rest. They explore a quieter setting.

**Try one step**

Identify what is useful, what gets in the way, and an adjustable setting or boundary in a fictional situation.

**Look more closely**

Could a change preserve needed connection or accessibility?

**A possible next use**

Plan one reversible change and a time to review it.

**Scope and limits**

Screen time alone does not describe wellbeing. This self-check is not a diagnosis; unsafe online behavior may need adult support.

## Disability Voices

Tool ID: `disabilityVoices`.

**What you can explore**

Learn from disabled people's accounts while attending to differences between voices.

**A worked example**

A learner asks of an advocate's account, "What barrier are they naming, and what change are they asking for?"

**Try one step**

Summarize one argument in your own words and identify its original source and context.

**Look more closely**

What assumption did it challenge? Where might another disabled person disagree?

**A possible next use**

Connect an access issue to a concrete classroom or community change.

**Scope and limits**

One advocate cannot represent all disabled people. A short quote is not a complete position.

## Ecomap

Tool ID: `ecomap`.

**What you can explore**

Explore how settings and systems support or strain participation.

**A worked example**

School offers a fictional learner friendship and a noisy lunch space. One connection can contain support and strain.

**Try one step**

Map one fictional system and describe flows of time, help, demands, or resources.

**Look more closely**

What could the system change, rather than asking only the individual to cope?

**A possible next use**

Name an access question or resource to discuss with a support person.

**Scope and limits**

A map is a selected perspective, not a clinical assessment or a complete picture of a system.

## Emotions Explorer

Tool ID: `emotions`.

**What you can explore**

Build feeling vocabulary while leaving room for uncertainty and mixtures.

**A worked example**

Starting a new activity, a character says, "I might be curious and nervous at the same time."

**Try one step**

Compare two feeling words for a fictional situation. Explain why either might fit.

**Look more closely**

What context would distinguish the words? Could neither fit?

**A possible next use**

Use a word, picture, or "not sure" to begin a future check-in.

**Scope and limits**

Faces, body language, and ratings do not reveal another person's feelings with certainty.

## Ethical Reasoning Lab

Tool ID: `ethicalReasoning`.

**What you can explore**

Explain a decision through reasons, stakeholder perspectives, and tradeoffs.

**A worked example**

A club compares equal funding shares with extra support for members facing access barriers.

**Try one step**

Compare two approaches to a fictional dilemma and explain whose needs each prioritizes.

**Look more closely**

What does your preferred approach overlook? What fact could change your position?

**A possible next use**

Practice giving a reason and acknowledging uncertainty in a discussion.

**Scope and limits**

An argument or quiz score is not a measure of moral worth. Reasonable disagreement may remain.

## Executive Function

Tool ID: `execfunction`.

**What you can explore**

Match a task barrier with a practical external support.

**A worked example**

A partner helps a character turn "work on the assignment" into "open the instructions and mark one question."

**Try one step**

Identify whether starting, remembering, sequencing, or estimating time is the immediate barrier in a fictional task.

**Look more closely**

Does the support reduce that barrier or simply ask for more effort?

**A possible next use**

Try a cue, checklist, model, or support request and review its fit.

**Scope and limits**

Task difficulty is not laziness. Strategies do not replace needed accommodations or support.

## Friendship Workshop

Tool ID: `friendship`.

**What you can explore**

Practice invitations, reciprocity, and boundaries without requiring closeness.

**A worked example**

A character invites someone to a game and says, "Okay, you're welcome another time," when they decline.

**Try one step**

Rehearse an invitation and responses to yes, no, and not now using a fictional pair.

**Look more closely**

Does each person have a real choice? What shows interest without pressure?

**A possible next use**

Choose a respectful way to check availability before an invitation.

**Scope and limits**

Friendship is not earned by compliance. Ending contact or seeking support can be appropriate when boundaries are ignored.

## Genogram

Tool ID: `genogram`.

**What you can explore**

Use a family map to organize chosen information and questions.

**A worked example**

A fictional map leaves an unknown relationship unfilled instead of guessing or forcing a standard family shape.

**Try one step**

Map a small fictional family or use roles. Mark what is known, unknown, or intentionally private.

**Look more closely**

What does the map show, and what would be unfair to infer?

**A possible next use**

Formulate a question or support need, only if sharing feels appropriate.

**Scope and limits**

Family structure does not establish causes, diagnoses, or future behavior. You may skip family history entirely.

## Goal Setter

Tool ID: `goals`.

**What you can explore**

Connect a meaningful goal to a manageable action and useful supports.

**A worked example**

A learner replaces "get better at reading" with "try a short section with audio and notice what helps."

**Try one step**

For a fictional goal, name a first step, a support, and a way to notice what happened.

**Look more closely**

Would you keep, change, shrink, or pause the step?

**A possible next use**

Choose when to revisit the plan and what help to request.

**Scope and limits**

Checked steps, ratings, and XP are activity records, not measures of learning or worth.

## Grief & Loss

Tool ID: `griefLoss`.

**What you can explore**

Explore ways people can make room for loss and receive support.

**A worked example**

A fictional character sometimes wants to remember a lost pet and sometimes wants an ordinary activity. Both preferences can be acknowledged.

**Try one step**

Read one reflection or support option and choose whether to explore, use fiction, or pass.

**Look more closely**

Does it allow different feelings and timing?

**A possible next use**

Identify a comforting routine or someone who can listen without requiring a particular response.

**Scope and limits**

There is no required sequence or deadline for grief. No personal loss story is needed here.

## Growth Mindset Workshop

Tool ID: `growthmindset`.

**What you can explore**

Connect learning with strategies, feedback, access, and realistic next steps.

**A worked example**

A character says, "This explanation did not work for me. I can ask for a different example and try one part."

**Try one step**

Replace a fictional all-or-nothing statement with a specific difficulty and possible change in approach.

**Look more closely**

What support is needed beyond effort? Would changing the task or taking a break help?

**A possible next use**

Choose a feedback question that helps decide what to try next.

**Scope and limits**

Growth does not mean unlimited improvement through effort alone. Barriers and support needs are real.

## Healthy Relationships

Tool ID: `healthyRelationships`.

**What you can explore**

Identify consent, respect, and access to support in relationship situations.

**A worked example**

A character declines to share a private message. A respectful response accepts the boundary without punishment.

**Try one step**

Compare a response that respects a fictional boundary with one that adds pressure.

**Look more closely**

Can the person change their mind freely? Who could help if the boundary is ignored?

**A possible next use**

Rehearse a support-seeking question or locate help information.

**Scope and limits**

A checklist cannot establish safety. Coercion or danger should not become a shared negotiation task.

## HOWL Tracker

Tool ID: `howlTracker`.

**What you can explore**

Connect a work-and-learning self-rating to evidence and conditions.

**A worked example**

A learner says, "I contributed more when I had questions ahead of time." The support matters alongside the rating.

**Try one step**

Describe an example of a work-and-learning habit and a condition that helped or hindered it.

**Look more closely**

Would a different setting change the rating? What does the number leave out?

**A possible next use**

Choose a support or small practice to discuss at a future check-in.

**Scope and limits**

Ratings are contextual self-reports, not fixed traits or measures of obedience.

## Identity Support

Tool ID: `identitySupport`.

**What you can explore**

Explore language and support while keeping identity and disclosure self-directed.

**A worked example**

A character is unsure which label fits and learns more without deciding or telling anyone today.

**Try one step**

Explore a term or support option. Note a question, using a fictional example if preferred.

**Look more closely**

Does the information allow uncertainty and self-description?

**A possible next use**

Identify a trusted resource or a boundary about what stays private.

**Scope and limits**

No label, coming-out step, or disclosure is required. Others cannot determine your identity from an activity.

## Feelings Journal

Tool ID: `journal`.

**What you can explore**

Notice context, supports, and possible next steps through reflection.

**A worked example**

A learner writes, "The room was loud. A quieter seat helped me start. Next time I can ask before the task."

**Try one step**

Choose a prompt or fictional event. Record only useful detail, or respond away from the form.

**Look more closely**

What helped, what remains uncertain, and what needs adapting?

**A possible next use**

Revisit a note in a similar situation and keep or revise the plan.

**Scope and limits**

A mood trend is not a complete account of wellbeing. An activity note differs from a saved or shared project copy.

## MAPS

Tool ID: `maps`.

**What you can explore**

Explore a person's aspirations, strengths, needs, and first steps with their direction.

**A worked example**

A fictional learner discusses a hope and support need while passing on a prompt about difficult experiences.

**Try one step**

Start with one chosen prompt. Connect an aspiration to a needed support or resource.

**Look more closely**

Whose priorities drive the plan? Can the person revise or decline it?

**A possible next use**

## Mindfulness Corner

Tool ID: `mindfulness`.

**What you can explore**

Practice choosing and returning attention in a comfortable way.

**A worked example**

A character notices a sound, becomes distracted, and returns to looking at an object without calling distraction failure.

**Try one step**

Read a practice and choose a comfortable focus, an observation-only version, or a pass.

**Look more closely**

What was noticing attention shifts like? Did the focus fit?

**A possible next use**

Choose a brief attention practice or another support for a future pause.

**Scope and limits**

Eyes may stay open and movement is allowed. Stop uncomfortable exercise; stillness or calm is not proof of success.

## Motivational Interviewing

Tool ID: `motivationalInterviewing`.

**What you can explore**

Practice listening that supports a person's own reasons and choices.

**A worked example**

A listener says, "You want more time with friends, and changing the routine feels difficult," then checks their understanding.

**Try one step**

For a fictional change, write an open question and a reflection that does not push a preferred answer.

**Look more closely**

Is there room for mixed feelings and deciding not to change?

**A possible next use**

Use a permission question before offering ideas in a conversation.

**Scope and limits**

These skills do not make someone a counselor and should not be used to steer others covertly.

## One-Page Profile

Tool ID: `onePageProfile`.

**What you can explore**

Describe what matters to a person and what useful support looks like.

**A worked example**

A profile says, "Give me written steps and time for a question," instead of "needs to focus more."

**Try one step**

For a fictional person, write a strength, a priority, and a concrete support instruction.

**Look more closely**

Could someone act on the wording? Has the person approved sharing it?

**A possible next use**

Review the profile with its owner before a transition or handoff.

**Scope and limits**

The person chooses content and audience. A profile should not become a fixed label or replace asking them.

## Orientations

Tool ID: `orientations`.

**What you can explore**

Compare philosophical responses while recognizing contexts and limits.

Two perspectives on a busy life emphasize personal choices and responsibilities to others. A learner examines what each highlights.

## Peer Support Coach

Tool ID: `peersupport`.

**What you can explore**

Offer attentive listening and connect someone with further help when needed.

**A worked example**

A peer asks, "Would you like me to listen or help find someone to talk to?" without promising secrecy.

**Try one step**

Practice an open question, a reflection, and a support-seeking phrase using a fictional situation.

**Look more closely**

## PERMA Wellbeing

Tool ID: `perma`.

**What you can explore**

Explore areas of experience without reducing wellbeing to a score.

**A worked example**

A character reports connection in a club and difficulty resting. Both can coexist without becoming a judgment about their life.

**Try one step**

Choose a domain. Give a fictional example of a support and a barrier within it.

**Look more closely**

What does a rating capture, and what context would you add?

**A possible next use**

Choose an accessible opportunity or support to explore in that domain.

**Scope and limits**

This adapted self-check is not a clinical measure or a requirement to feel positive in every domain.

## Perspective-Taking Lab

Tool ID: `perspective`.

**What you can explore**

Separate observation from interpretation and ask about missing context.

**A worked example**

A classmate has not replied. A character considers being busy, missing the message, or needing space as possible explanations.

**Try one step**

Name an observable fact, two plausible interpretations, and a respectful question about a fictional scene.

**Look more closely**

How could you check an interpretation without demanding private information?

**A possible next use**

Use "I noticed... I wondered..." for an appropriate clarification.

**Scope and limits**

Perspective-taking is not mind-reading. Understanding a viewpoint does not require agreeing with harmful behavior.

## Quiet Questions

Tool ID: `quietQuestions`.

**What you can explore**

Explore an open question without rushing to a final answer.

**A worked example**

A character asks, "When can I ask for help?" Their answer changes after a different classroom experience.

**Try one step**

Choose a question and notice an example, counterexample, or further question. A brief thought or a pass is enough.

**Look more closely**

Did context change your thinking? Is leaving the question open useful?

**A possible next use**

Return at a time you choose and notice what differs.

**Scope and limits**

No insight, belief, daily streak, or week-long commitment is required for your own inquiry.

## Restorative Circle

Tool ID: `restorativeCircle`.

**What you can explore**

Plan a voluntary conversation about participation, needs, and repair.

**A worked example**

A facilitator explains the purpose, models passing, and asks how participants want to contribute.

**Try one step**

Plan a fictional community-building circle with an invitation, neutral prompt, pass option, and closing check.

**Look more closely**

Who has power? What makes participation meaningfully voluntary?

**A possible next use**

Identify a facilitation support or access adjustment before using a circle.

**Scope and limits**

A circle does not replace safeguarding. No one must forgive, disclose harm, or meet someone who harmed them.

## Safety & Boundaries

Tool ID: `safety`.

**What you can explore**

Practice recognizing a boundary and asking for help.

**A worked example**

A character says, "I do not want that photo shared," then asks a trusted adult for help when ignored.

**Try one step**

For a fictional scenario, identify a boundary phrase and a support person or route.

**Look more closely**

What if the first person does not help?

**A possible next use**

Rehearse another help request using words, pointing, writing, signing, or AAC.

**Scope and limits**

A child is not responsible for preventing another person's harmful actions. Help-seeking does not require perfect words.

## CBT Thought Record

Tool ID: `thoughtRecord`.

**What you can explore**

Distinguish a situation, an interpretation, and what evidence supports.

**A worked example**

A character thinks, "One unanswered question means I am bad at this." They notice one difficulty does not establish overall ability.

**Try one step**

Separate fact from thought in a low-stakes fictional event, then consider a more precise interpretation.

**Look more closely**

Does the alternative fit evidence without denying a real barrier or harm?

**A possible next use**

Choose a question such as "What else do I know?" for later reflection.

**Scope and limits**

The aim is accuracy, not forced positive thinking. This exercise does not replace support or therapy.

## Voice Detective

Tool ID: `voicedetective`.

**What you can explore**

Notice vocal cues while keeping interpretations tentative.

**A worked example**

A short phrase can sound different with context, fatigue, accent, or recording conditions. A listener considers more than one meaning.

**Try one step**

Compare a sample's words and audible features. Offer a possible interpretation and a question to check it.

**Look more closely**

What did you hear, and what did you infer? What is missing?

**A possible next use**

Use clarification rather than treating tone as proof in real conversations.

**Scope and limits**

Voice alone cannot establish emotion, honesty, or intent. Learners can explore context and wording without completing an audio task.

## Emotion Zones

Tool ID: `zones`.

**What you can explore**

Use zone words or colors to describe possible feelings and support needs.

**A worked example**

A character feels tired and worried together. They choose a useful description and ask for a manageable first step.

**Try one step**

Compare two zone descriptions for a fictional situation, or choose unsure. Name a possible support.

**Look more closely**

Does the color help, or would a word, picture, or another description work better?

**A possible next use**

Practice a support request tied to the situation rather than a demand to change color.

**Scope and limits**

No zone makes someone good or bad. Reaching green is not a condition for inclusion or support.
