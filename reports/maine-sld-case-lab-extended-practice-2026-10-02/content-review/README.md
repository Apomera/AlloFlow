## Material design choices

- Marisol, Grade4, uses Spanish and English. Her family describes assets and reading concerns; schooling and language-access records are incomplete. The English-only assessment lacks a documented suitability review, so no standardized scores are supplied. This is uncertainty about this excerpt, not a blanket prohibition on multilingual learners' standardized testing. Q1-Q4/Q7-Q9 remain training-only More evidence responses; Q5/Q6 stay narratives. The guide encourages timely appropriate evaluation alongside continuing supports, without a language-proficiency waiting rule.
- Taylor, Grade5, has a complete, valid reevaluation stipulated to establish continuing SLD and necessary disability-related instructional adaptations. Independent reading remains inadequate for age/grade while accessible content work and grades improve, avoiding a contradictory Q1 YES premise. Four generic fictional age-based achievement standard scores illustrate the record; they are not a branded battery or eligibility thresholds. Q9a is supported by the full record's necessity finding, not by progress, intensity, staff/setting or isolated scores alone. No support withdrawal is proposed.

## Primary-source verification

1. [Current Maine DOE SLD form/checklist](https://www.maine.gov/doe/sites/maine.gov.doe/files/inline-files/Supervision%20and%20Monitoring%20-%20Specific%20Learner%20Disability%20Checklist%20-%208.13.2026.pdf), retrieved October2. Q1 requires multiple valid/reliable sources; Q2 reviews appropriate opportunity to learn; Q3 asks primary cause; Q4 considers four evidence streams; Q5/Q6 are narratives; Q7 reviews assessment validity/qualifications; Q8/Q9 distinguish SLD from required instruction. More evidence is a training response, not an official additional checkbox. The form file includes revision text effective10/25/2023 within its2026 checklist distribution; the draft does not invent a new effective date.
2. [MUSER Chapter101 current2026 DOCX](https://www.maine.gov/sos/sites/maine.gov.sos/files/inline-files/071c101-2026-129-AMD-EMR.docx), retrieved October2. Verified VII.2.L(2)(a)-(c): appropriate sources/pattern and primary-cause review; documented instruction/repeated achievement assessment; performance/work-sample approaches where applicable norms are unavailable. Verified X.2.A(2) SDI definition: disability-related adaptations of content, methodology or delivery enabling curriculum access/standards. Necessary-instruction dependence is a scenario stipulation and inference from that definition and Q9, not a numerical rule in MUSER.
3. [Maine DOE multilingual guide](https://www.maine.gov/doe/sites/maine.gov.doe/files/inline-files/Multilingual%20Learning%20-%20Identifying%20%26%20Serving%20Students%20Who%20are%20Multilingual%20with%20Disabilities%20Policy%20%26%20Resource%20Guide%20-%204.14.2025.pdf), verified live October2, pp8,20-26,33-35,38. It supports language/acquisition expertise, culturally/linguistically responsive evaluation and family interpretation; prohibits language-proficiency waiting policies and exclusion of dual ESOL/special-education services. Its existing [DOE resource page](https://www.maine.gov/doe/learning/multilinguallearner/resources/MLdisabilities) still links the guide. No ACCESS cutoff or English-learning duration is invented.
4. [IDEA300.304(c)](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/d/300.304/c/): assessment in native language/communication mode unless clearly infeasible; appropriate, valid/reliable use and qualified administration. [IDEA300.306](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/d/300.306): inappropriate instruction/limited English proficiency cannot be the determinant factor for eligibility and varied sources must be considered. [IDEA300.39(b)(3)](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/a/300.39/b/3): SDI adapts instruction for disability-related needs/curriculum access.
