# Federal IDEA foundation and Maine procedure

Jurisdiction review: October 4, 2026. This is a focused review of the activity's federal/state scope; it does not replace the existing case-content review or revalidate every fictional score, route, or worksheet field.

The activity applies federal IDEA principles through Maine's MUSER and Maine DOE SLD form. The evidence-reasoning skills are useful beyond Maine. Its nine-question form, branches, and Maine-specific identification procedure should not be presented as a national eligibility workflow. Federal rules require public agencies to use the SLD criteria adopted by their state. [34 CFR 300.307](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/d/300.307)

## Verified crosswalk

| Topic | Federal foundation | Maine procedure represented in the lab |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disability and instruction need | IDEA eligibility includes a qualifying disability and a need for special education because of it. Specially designed instruction addresses disability-related needs through adaptations in content, methodology, or delivery. | Maine's special-education definition in MUSER II.37 uses that federal foundation. The lab separately asks for the SLD conclusion and instruction required. |
| Evaluation | Evaluation uses varied sources, including family input; no single measure is the sole eligibility criterion. Assessment selection, administration, language, validity, and qualifications matter. | The case and score record, classroom and intervention evidence, family input, and multilingual validity questions practice these principles. |
| SLD identification | Underachievement in the applicable academic area, insufficient intervention progress or a relevant strengths-and-weaknesses pattern under adopted state criteria, appropriate instruction, and primary-cause considerations are addressed. | MUSER VII.2.L uses a relevant strengths-and-weaknesses pattern and requires considering intervention response, classroom performance, achievement, and psychological-processing data. |
| State methods | State criteria must permit an intervention-response process and must not require a severe discrepancy between intellectual ability and achievement. Agencies follow their state criteria. | The Maine form and its Q1–Q9 branches are the procedure practiced here. They are not a federally prescribed nine-question form. |
| Adverse effect | Achievement, educational needs, and special-education need remain relevant to SLD eligibility. | MUSER VII.3 explicitly excludes SLD from its separate adverse-effect determination procedure. This exception does not make educational impact irrelevant. |
| Observation and documentation | IDEA includes learning-environment observation and specific eligibility documentation, including each member's certification or separate conclusions. | MUSER VII.2.L contains corresponding observation and documentation requirements; the lab summarizes the official form for training. |
