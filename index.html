From source to student-ready resource

Keep the science. Change the support.

One water-cycle passage can support shorter sentences, a focused glossary, and more than one way to explain an idea.

Illustrative example: these samples were prepared for this page. They are not a live AI result, a reading-level assessment, or a record of a real student.

1 · Bring a source

A shared learning goal

Explain how the Sun helps water move through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection.

Sample source passage

Energy from the Sun warms water in lakes and oceans. During evaporation, liquid water changes into water vapor, a gas. As moist air cools, water vapor can condense into tiny liquid droplets that form clouds. When droplets grow large enough, they fall as rain, a form of precipitation. Water collects in lakes, rivers, and oceans, where it can evaporate again. This describes one possible path through the water cycle.

Download sample text (.txt)

To try it: open AlloFlow, paste this source, and choose a resource. AI generation uses your selected access path.

2 · Choose a support

Explore three sample resources

Shorter sentences, same process terms

The Sun warms water in lakes and oceans. Some liquid water becomes water vapor, a gas. This is evaporation. Moist air can cool. Water vapor can then become tiny liquid drops. This is condensation. The drops form clouds. When drops grow large enough, they fall as rain. Rain is one kind of precipitation. Water gathers in lakes, rivers, and oceans. This is collection. It can evaporate again. Water can follow other paths, too.

Teacher decision: keep the scientific vocabulary and the idea of a cycle. Check whether shorter sentences help the learners; no grade-level match is assumed.

A small glossary with familiar examples
Evaporation
Liquid water changes into water vapor. Example: a puddle gets smaller as water evaporates.
Condensation
Water vapor changes into liquid water. Example: drops form on the outside of a cold glass.
Precipitation
Water falls from clouds, for example as rain or snow.
Collection
Water gathers in places such as lakes, rivers, and oceans.

Teacher decision: preview the terms needed for this lesson. Explain that the drops on a cold glass come from water vapor in the surrounding air.

One exit ticket, different response options

Prompt: How does the Sun help water move through the water cycle? Use the words evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection. Explain one possible path.

Response options: write a short explanation, explain aloud, or label a diagram and describe the connections using an established communication method.

Teacher decision: apply the same science criteria to each response. Check the Sun's role, changes between liquid and gas, and connections between stages. Do not score handwriting or speaking fluency as science understanding.

The teacher makes the final call.

Compare each resource with the source, check the science, and try the support in the learner's actual reading or response workflow.

Equity First

Local-First Architecture

AlloFlow is built around a local-first app shell. While AI generation usually requires a provider connection, exported project and HTML bundles can preserve many study resources for offline or low-connectivity use after the materials are created.

🌐 Online: AI Generation
Generating new worlds, quizzes, or rewritten text uses the selected AI provider. Cloud providers need a network connection; local models need a configured runtime.
⚡ Offline: Interactive Play
After assets are loaded, many STEM, game, Word Sounds, and Immersive Reader interactions can run locally in the browser. Cloud AI generation and hosted-session features remain network-dependent.
📥 The Bridge: HTML Bundles
Supported resources can be exported as HTML for offline use. Open the exported file on the student device and test the activities; external media, AI generation, and connected features may still need a network.

AlloFlow supports model-agnostic AI backends. AlloFlow Desktop is the normal local-first teacher-laptop path. The Docker School Box stack is optional server/appliance infrastructure for schools that need district-owned local services.

🐳 Optional Docker School Box
docker compose up -d launches local services such as PocketBase, Ollama, Piper / Edge TTS, SearXNG, Nginx, and optional Flux image generation when GPU hardware is available.
🔒 School-Controlled Infrastructure
When configured for local providers, AI and data services can run on school-controlled hardware. FERPA posture still depends on district policy, contracts, retention, and configuration.
🔌 Pluggable Backends
Choose your AI provider: Gemini, Ollama, OpenAI, Claude, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Switch backends in one click, no code changes for supported provider configurations.

Simple, Transparent Access

AlloFlow has no software license fee and is open source. The web Gemini Canvas path may require an eligible Google account; Desktop and local-provider paths can run without one.

Keyless web path

Gemini Canvas

No AlloFlow license fee. Google controls account eligibility, availability, quotas, and plan terms; school accounts may require administrator enablement.

Recommended local-first path

AlloFlow Desktop

No AlloFlow license fee. Local models avoid cloud API charges; hardware, electricity, support, and any optional cloud provider remain your responsibility.

School-controlled deployment

Hosted or School Box

No AlloFlow license fee. Budget separately for hosting or hardware, security operations, support, and usage-based AI services.

Data Privacy & Architecture

AlloFlow is designed with security in mind, putting control in the hands of educators.

File-Based Persistence

Use Save Project to keep a JSON copy you can reopen or share. Available recovery and storage depend on the workspace and device profile; clearing browser data or resetting a device can remove local work. Read the saving and recovery guide.

Google Education Infrastructure

The Gemini Canvas path uses the Gemini access available to your Google account. Your district should verify which services and features its Google Workspace for Education agreements cover, along with account settings, data handling, and retention. Opening AlloFlow inside Canvas does not itself establish approval for student data. Compare data paths and setup requirements.

Local-First Student Data

Student progress, XP, quiz scores, and session data can stay on-device in local-first paths. Cloud, hosted-session, and provider-backed paths vary by deployment. Teachers can export data as JSON files for record-keeping. The Student Portal uses generated codenames to reduce the need for real names. Codenames do not make linked work or session records anonymous.
