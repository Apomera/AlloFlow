Stages observed in the current investigation: 4/4
1. Crystal violet: Model A — Purple; Model B — Purple. The primary dye colors both models purple. This stage does not distinguish their wall types.
2. Iodine: Model A — Purple; Model B — Purple. Iodine forms a complex with crystal violet. Both models remain purple.
3. Decolorization: Model A — Purple; Model B — Colorless. Decolorization dehydrates thick peptidoglycan, helping retain the violet–iodine complex. In the thin-wall model, disruption of the outer membrane and the thin peptidoglycan layer allow the complex to wash out.
4. Safranin counterstain: Model A — Purple; Model B — Pink. The counterstain colors the decolorized model pink. The retained purple dye masks the counterstain in the thick-wall model.

A Gram result cannot identify a species or establish antibiotic susceptibility. Real samples may stain variably because of cell condition or technique; some cell envelopes need other staining methods.
