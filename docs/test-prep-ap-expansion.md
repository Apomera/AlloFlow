## 2026-09-06 review addendum

Measured from the shipped files, not from this document: eight internal AP packs (Psychology 500, Biology 500, U.S. History 600, U.S. Government 260, Statistics 240, Chemistry 700, Physics 1 500, Calculus AB 160; 3,460 single-choice items). Manifest counts match the pack files. The AP Statistics foundation pilot (five units, 55 public framework topics, nine unscored workshops, five quick-reference cards, four study routes) was not previously listed here.

**Study-plan layers were authored but never rendered.** The AP Physics 1 library (v0.19) carries topic diagnostic routes, unit review routes, review ladders, practice forms, misconception families and playbooks, study session plans, spaced-review plans, mastery checkpoints, adaptive review queues, and a learner-evidence schema; AP Calculus AB carries topic diagnostic routes and topic remediation playbooks; every pack carries a source catalog. Until 2026-09-06 no Hub code path read any of these keys. The Hub now normalizes both authoring shapes through `testPrepStudyPlanLayers` and renders four library modes: Study plan (topic routes with entry/reinforcement/transfer or foundation/depth/transfer sets, unit reviews, ladders, forms), Misconception playbooks, Study sessions (session plans and the spaced-review cadence table), and Sources. Route and set progress, a deterministic start/continue/retry pointer, and last-practiced time are derived from local practice history through the same derivation the Government document routes use. Mastery checkpoints, adaptive review queues, and the learner-evidence schema remain unrendered on purpose: they describe transitions driven by evidence stages the Hub does not yet capture, and rendering them as computed would be a readiness claim.

**Seven of eight packs now declare a topic universe.** Calculus AB (66), Statistics (55), Government (60), and U.S. History (105) already did. On 2026-09-06 the public framework topic numbering was transcribed from the official Course and Exam Descriptions for Biology (60 topics, Effective Fall 2025), Chemistry (91, Effective Fall 2024), and Psychology (35, Effective Fall 2025), verified against each pack's own item topic ids, and recorded in `dev-tools/ap_framework_topics.cjs` and each pack's `blueprint.officialFrameworkTopicIds`. All three matched their packs exactly, so the declaration records existing scope rather than changing it. Only the numbering is stored; no CED titles, learning objectives, or other prose are reproduced.

**AP Physics 1 numbers topics on its own scheme, and now has a draft crosswalk.** It is the one pack that cannot declare a framework universe, and the reason is a real defect rather than a missing field. Checked against the AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based CED (Effective Fall 2024, 43 framework topics), the pack's ids look like framework ids but do not index the same topics: its 2.6 "Circular Motion" is framework 2.9, its 3.4 "Power" is framework 3.5, its 2.4 "Gravitational Force" is framework 2.6, and it carries a topic 4.5 "Collisions" where framework Unit 4 ends at 4.4. Thirteen items route to that 4.5. The pack records `blueprint.topicIdScheme: "internal-pack-numbering"` so its coverage reads as unmeasured instead of silently passing.

`dev-tools/ap_physics_1_topic_crosswalk.cjs` now maps the pack's 39 internal topics onto the 43 framework topics by title, carrying a confidence on every row: 19 exact, 12 high, 7 inferred, and 1 unmapped (the pack's "Drag Forces", which is not a separate framework topic). The mapping reaches 37 of 43 framework topics and leaves **six** with no pack topic behind them: 2.8 Spring Forces, 3.4 Conservation of Energy, 5.2 Connecting Linear and Rotational Motion, 5.6 Newton's Second Law in Rotational Form, 6.2 Torque and Work, and 6.6 Motion of Orbiting Satellites.

Two structural problems surface in the same pass: pack topics 2.3 ("Newton Laws") and 3.3 ("Work-Energy Theorem") each span several framework topics, so neither establishes coverage of any one of them; and framework topics 2.1, 3.1, 3.2, and 6.3 are each claimed by two different pack topics. Both need resolving alongside the missing six.

The crosswalk is a draft pending AP Physics subject-expert review, and says so in its own boundary field: the `inferred` rows are judgement calls, and no coverage figure derived from it may be published or shown to a learner until that review is recorded. Comparing raw ids across the two numbering schemes produces a different and wrong uncovered list, which is precisely the error the crosswalk exists to prevent.

**One QA contract.** The eight AP QA reports previously published three shapes (`editorialAdvisories`/`signals`, `editorialReviewQueue`, `advisories`/`coverage`), and the three `coverage` blocks disagreed with one another (booleans in Statistics, raw count maps in Government), so a passing report meant something different in each. Every AP QA report now also carries `blueprintCoverage` from `dev-tools/ap_blueprint_coverage_core.cjs`: unit and topic coverage against the declared universe (explicitly null, and flagged `topic-universe-not-declared`, where none is declared), off-blueprint topic routing, minimum and median items per declared topic, answer-position balance, per-layer library counts, and the list of empty layers. It is structural coverage only and states that boundary in the block itself. Current results: four packs `structurally-complete`, four `gaps-present` solely because they declare no topic universe; all eight represent every declared unit, route nothing off blueprint, and hold answer keys at an even 25 percent per position.

**Cited AP sources are now named for learners.** Twenty public sources cited by the Calculus AB, Chemistry, Physics 1, Statistics, and Government packs had no entry in `test_prep/reference_catalog.json`, because the catalog builder walks `*_pack.json` and the AP packs deliberately do not use that suffix. The entries now live in `dev-tools/ap_reference_sources.cjs`, shared by the builder, and each names the College Board course, exam, and framework documents plus the OpenStax cross-check texts with an explicit statement that OpenStax is not a College Board publication and does not define the framework. This matters more now that the Hub renders a Sources view from the same URLs.

**Library depth is uneven against bank size.** Chemistry (700 items) has nine flashcards, nine memory aids, no diagrams, and no workshops; Biology (500 items) has eight and eight with no diagrams or workshops; Government has no diagrams; Calculus AB carries the richest library but the thinnest bank at 160 items. Only U.S. History ships a glossary although the Hub renders one for any pack that has one. Psychology and U.S. History omit top-level `examModes` and `responseTypes`.

The sharpest measure of that gap was lesson depth: Biology and Chemistry gave each unit exactly **one** native lesson section, while every other pack gives three, so each unit routed sixty to eighty items at a single lesson. It is tracked by `lessonDepth.sectionsPerChapter` in every AP QA report.

**Biology now has three sections per unit.** On 2026-09-06, `dev-tools/ap_biology_lesson_sections.cjs` added two original sections to each of the eight units, split by public framework topic (for example Unit 3 now has Enzymes and pathways, Photosynthesis, and Cellular respiration and fermentation). Each new section carries the same structure as the existing rich lessons: examples, nonexamples and boundaries, a common misconception, a worked data table that separates what an observation supports from what it does not establish, retrieval prompts, a transfer move, and a section-bound knowledge check. All 500 items now route to the section that owns their topic rather than to one unit lesson; every section receives items and no route is dead. The eight original chapter checks, which all keyed at position A, now key across all four positions. The library is 24 sections and 24 knowledge checks, and the QA gate was moved from 8/8/1 to 24/24/3 in the same change.

**Chemistry now has three sections per unit as well.** `dev-tools/ap_chemistry_lesson_sections.cjs` added two original sections to each of the nine units by the same recipe (for example Unit 8 now has Proton transfer and aqueous equilibria, Acid-base titrations and the molecular structure of acids, and Buffers: pKa, Henderson-Hasselbalch, capacity, and solubility). All 700 items route to the section owning their topic, every one of the 27 sections receives items, the nine original chapter checks that all keyed at A now spread across positions, and the QA gate moved from 9/9/1 to 27/27/3. Every AP pack now reports three lesson sections per unit.

**Written-response workshops: a crash fixed and two packs filled.** The Hub's workshop view mapped over `taskParts`, `planningFrame`, `successCriteria`, `commonPitfalls`, and `sampleOutline` without guards, and only the Government and Psychology packs author those fields. Physics 1 (8 workshops) uses `scenario`, `parts`, `scoringGuide`, and `selfCheck`; Calculus AB (8) uses `scenario`, `parts`, and `responsePlanning` with no prompt; Statistics (9) and U.S. History (3) carry a prompt and a `selfCheck` list. So opening the view in any of those four packs threw on the first record, and 28 of the 35 authored AP workshops were unreachable. `testPrepNormalizeWorkshop` now folds all four shapes into the rendered record (parts become task parts, `lookFor` becomes a planning frame, the scoring guide becomes success criteria, self-check lists are shown as such, calculator and time notes become a badge), and every list in the view is guarded so a shape that lacks one simply omits it. Biology and Chemistry, which had none, gained one original unscored planning workshop per unit (8 and 9) in the rendered shape at the depth the Government gate requires: an original synthetic stimulus, three task parts, a four-step planning frame, four success criteria, four common pitfalls, and a three-point sample outline, with the same unscored, unofficial, release-blocked flags and rights and accessibility records as the other packs. Both QA gates now require that depth. Diagrams remain the last empty layer in those two libraries.

**Cross-references resolve.** Every id the study-plan view turns into navigation is now checked: item learning sections and chapters, and route, diagnostic-set, unit-review, ladder, form, and session item, section, chapter, flashcard, memory-aid, and knowledge-check references. Across the eight packs 10,350 references are checked and none is unresolved. The check is calibrated against corrupted input for each reference kind, so a passing result is evidence rather than silence.

**Sequencing.** Before a ninth subject: put the draft AP Physics 1 crosswalk through subject-expert review, then author items for its six uncovered framework topics and split its two multi-topic pack topics; raise Biology and Chemistry to three lesson sections per unit and give them diagrams and workshops; and land schema-v2 stimulus groups (still `stimulusGroupsIncluded: false` in all eight). Cheapest expansions afterward are Calculus BC, World History: Modern, Human Geography, and Environmental Science; English Language and Literature, Economics, and Computer Science wait on stimulus, diagram, and code response types.

## 2026-09-07 addendum: item quality

A scan of every shipped item bank (8 AP packs, 3,460 items; 32 packs in all) for cues a learner could use without knowing the content found three, and the Hub now neutralises the first in software while the QA contract names all three.

**Answer keys cycled A, B, C, D in file order.** Seven of the eight AP packs, and 22 of the 32 packs in the Hub, store their keys as `index % 4` (Biology, Chemistry, Physics 1, Calculus AB, Statistics, Government, and U.S. History at 100 percent; only Psychology, whose QA already carried an `answer-key-sequence` check, avoided it). The Hub's practice banks serve items in file order and never reordered choices, so the key for question 7 was always C. The Hub now presents each item's choices in a per-session order (`testPrepPresentItem`, seeded from the practice set and the item id) and maps the learner's pick back to the stored index before anything is scored or recorded, so progress, evidence, and content identity are unchanged. Order stays fixed when a pack or item declares `choicePresentation: 'fixed'`, when every choice is numeric (kept ascending, as on paper forms), when a choice refers to another choice (all of the above, I and II, both x and y), or when a rationale names a lettered option; the Statistics pack has six such items and Government none. Hands-free commands, the clarification prompt, and spoken feedback all work in the displayed order because they read the presented item.

**The longest choice is usually the key.** Counting only cases where the key is at least a quarter longer than the longest distractor: Government 63 percent, Physics 1 48 percent, Biology 39 percent, Statistics 34 percent, U.S. History 31 percent, Chemistry 29 percent. Psychology, whose QA has a `keyed-option-length-cues` check, sits at 6 percent. This is authoring work, not a rendering fix: distractors need the same specificity and length as keys.

**Three packs repeated one sentence for every distractor.** Statistics (720 of 720 distractor rationales), Government (780 of 780), and U.S. History (1,650 of 1,800) carried a generic "this choice does not match" sentence in place of option-level feedback, and the distractors themselves are almost all distinct (Government 765 of 780, U.S. History 1,799 of 1,800), so real feedback means item-by-item authoring. Government is done: `dev-tools/ap_us_government_distractor_feedback/` (one module per unit) holds 780 original sentences keyed by item id and distractor text; the builder refuses to build if any distractor lacks a match, the pack declares `capabilities.optionLevelFeedback: "authored-per-distractor"`, and its QA gained a `feedback-specificity` check (three distinct notes per item, none equal to the key rationale, none boilerplate), calibrated by injecting both defects and watching it fail. Statistics followed the same day: `dev-tools/ap_statistics_distractor_feedback/` (three part modules, 720 sentences; numeric distractors name the specific miscalculation that produces them, such as dividing by the sample size instead of its square root), the same build-time match requirement, the same capability flag, and a `feedback-specificity` finding in its QA, calibrated by injecting three defect types and getting exactly three findings. U.S. History, the largest bank, followed the same day: `dev-tools/ap_us_history_distractor_feedback/` (six part modules, 1,800 sentences) feeds both stages of its build (`build_ap_us_history_foundation.cjs` for items 1 to 50 and `build_ap_us_history_100_bank.cjs` for the extension), each of which now refuses to build without a match, and its QA carries the same `feedback-specificity` check, calibrated by injecting three defect types into three items, which produced four findings: the three specificity findings plus the deployment-parity finding that any edited pack triggers. A first draft of the U.S. History notes had 410 under thirty characters and 48 reused across items; every one was rewritten before shipping, and the QA gate now holds the floor. All three formerly boilerplate packs report 100 percent distinct distractor rationales. The Hub still collapses any repeated distractor note to a single line, which now applies to no shipped AP item.

**QA contract.** `blueprintCoverage.testWiseness` now reports, for every AP pack, the key-cycle share, the longest-key share (strict and by a quarter margin), the repeated-distractor-rationale share, and the largest set of items sharing one choice set, with advisories `answer-key-position-cycle`, `key-length-cue`, `distractor-rationales-repeated`, and `shared-choice-sets` (Psychology: 34 items share one research-methods choice set). Advisories do not fail QA: the position cue is neutralised at render time and the others are backlog, but a pack that clears one should see the advisory disappear.

## Decision

Preserve broad AP preparation as a Test Prep Hub expansion opportunity, but begin with one bounded pilot instead of creating a separate AP Psychology pathway or attempting all AP courses at once. Reuse the pack-agnostic Hub and retain its independent-content, transparent-evidence, accessibility, and no-official-score posture.

## Market hypothesis

Strong free AP resources already exist, but the learner experience is fragmented, some practice is teacher-assigned, and depth varies by subject. AlloFlow may fill a useful self-study gap by combining original blueprint-mapped practice, native chapters, flashcards, memory aids, transparent feedback and provenance, progress tools, and hands-free and large-text access in one workspace.

Treat this as a hypothesis. Re-check competitor coverage and pricing, College Board policy, current course and exam descriptions, and learner demand before making public market claims or funding a broad build-out.

## Constraints

- State clearly that AlloFlow is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by College Board. Do not claim official forms, official AP scores, score predictions, or equivalence to a released exam.
- Never copy secure or released questions. Author original material and use public course and exam descriptions only as blueprints; link learners to public official free-response materials where appropriate.
- Version every course against a named course and exam description and exam year. Blueprint drift triggers a new review rather than silently changing an existing pack.
- The current runtime is single-choice. Existing written-response workshops support planning and self-check; they are not automated FRQ scoring.
- The manifest-driven catalog and lazy CDN loader now keep AP assets outside the size-limited bundle. Broad expansion still requires schema-v2 stimulus and response types, catalog-scale usability testing, and per-course release gates.
- Visual and source-heavy courses require structured stimuli, meaningful alt text, accessible math, tables and media, rights review, and narration that does not erase information conveyed visually.
- Release requires original content, source review, accessibility QA, production validation, and independent course expertise. Psychometric or automated-writing claims require separate evidence.

## Phases

1. **Foundation (implemented):** Manifest-driven registration, lazy pack loading, schema/library validation, accessible failure states, explicit internal-QA controls, deterministic QA, and build-parity validation.
2. **Pilot (internal QA):** Original AP Psychology multiple-choice practice, native learning content, accessible diagram placements, clearly unscored FRQ planning workshops, the bounded AP Biology eight-unit foundation pilot, the AP U.S. History nine-period, 600-item five-item-per-topic foundation pilot, and the AP U.S. Government five-unit, 260-item all-topic foundation pilot with three practice angles per topic, five unscored response-planning workshops, and a thirteen-document 2026–27 public foundational-document crosswalk are implemented; all independent release gates remain open.
3. **Schema v2:** Add reusable stimulus groups; multi-select, numeric, short-response, and constructed-response records; accessible media, math, and tables; and rubric-grounded self-check.
4. **Course expansion:** Add subjects only when their current blueprint crosswalk, original item bank, learning library, rights and accessibility review, and subject-expert release gate are complete.

## Non-goals until evidence exists

- Official-equivalent forms or copied College Board content
- Scaled-score, pass, or college-credit predictions
- Automated FRQ scores
- A universal hands-free claim for content whose essential visual meaning is not yet represented accessibly
- Releasing all AP subjects before the pilot and shared architecture pass their gates

## Review triggers

Review this direction when College Board changes a course description or exam policy; the Hub schema, catalog, or loading model changes; new response types ship; a market claim is proposed; or an AP course moves from pilot to ready.
