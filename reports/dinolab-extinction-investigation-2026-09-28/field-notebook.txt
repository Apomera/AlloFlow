EXTINCTION INVESTIGATION | From impact to food webs
How could one impact affect life far from the crater?
Student writing is recorded without automatic assessment. The pathway is a simplified explanatory model, not a simulation.
First checked pathway
From impact to atmosphere: Only rocks touching the asteroid were affected.
From less light to producers: Every plant must die immediately.
From producers to food webs: Predators are unaffected because they do not eat plants.
Current pathway choices
From impact to atmosphere: Particles spread through the atmosphere and reduce sunlight.
From less light to producers: It can reduce photosynthesis and new food production.
From producers to food webs: Food shortages can spread through feeding relationships.
First recorded explanation
Cited sources:
A crater with a matching age [rock] | Renne and colleagues (2013) | https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1230492
A buried ~180 km crater near Mexico’s Yucatán is linked to an impact dated to 66.04 million years ago, with an uncertainty of about 40,000 years in a published estimate.
Limit: An age estimate is not an exact timestamp. A crater by itself does not describe the global ecological effects.
Testing an impact winter [model] | Senel and colleagues (2023) | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-023-01290-4
Climate simulations informed by geological evidence test how dust, soot, and sulfur-bearing particles could reduce sunlight and cool the surface.
Limit: Models test mechanisms; they are not direct observations of ancient weather. Particle amounts and sizes affect the predicted severity and duration.
My explanation: A local impact disrupted distant food webs.
How my sources connect: The crater supports an impact. Models connect atmospheric particles to reduced light and food production.
A limit or next question: This does not tell us exactly how long darkness lasted.
