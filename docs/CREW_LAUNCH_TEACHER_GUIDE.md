# Crew Launch: a teacher guide for the trimester

Twelve 40-minute Crew slots, one per week, built for King Middle School's three HOWLs. Each week is
one AlloFlow pack (a reading, words, a chart, a sort, a quiz, an SEL Hub tool, sentence frames, a
memory aid, and a challenge) plus one commitment the student carries into other classes and marks
off later in the week. This guide is one page per week: the shape of the 40 minutes, what students
do in the Hub, what the commitment is, what counts as HOWL evidence, and what tends to go wrong.

Written 2026-09-13 from the six packs in `allopacks/crew_*_grade6_8`. If you are new to the words
Crew, HOWL, or EL Education, read `docs/EL_CREW_PRIMER.md` first; it is one page.

| 7 | Two Sides of One Story | Respect | Perspective Lens, Peer Support Coach | Ask one question before deciding what someone meant |

The HOWL statements, from King's grading guide: Respect, "I am a respectful member of the King
community." Responsibility, "I take responsibility for my success as a learner." Perseverance, "I
persevere to produce high quality work."

## Before week 1: how the packs work

**Loading a pack.** Each week is one file. Open AlloFlow, open the History panel, and use Load
Project to open the week's `.allopack.json`. Once the packs are published to the Community Catalog
you will find them there under SEL / Character with the tag `crew`. Loading a pack puts nine
resources in the History panel and one Crew station under SEL Stations.

**Students do not need AI.** Portland students use AlloFlow without a Gemini key. Every resource in
these packs and every linked Hub tool works without one. Where a tool has an AI feature (Growth
Mindset's coach), it says so and points to the by-hand version.

**The directions resource is the run sheet.** Open it first. Steps 1 to 9 are in order and each
bold name is a resource in the History panel. Step 6 is a link into the SEL Hub; clicking it opens
the tool and starts the week's Crew station.

**The Crew station is where the week is recorded.** It has two steps. The first is five active
minutes in the week's tool, counted while the tab is visible and the student is using it. The
second is the commitment, which the student marks complete themselves later in the week ("Mark
complete"), or passes on for now. The station stays active until the student exits it, so it is
still there on Thursday. Students can find it again under SEL Stations in the History panel. The
record is a practice record, not a grade, and the station says so on screen. Saving the project
keeps it.

**What "HOWL evidence" means here.** Each week's commitment is one observable behavior from the
HOWL's own language on the grading guide. The evidence is the student's account of a specific
moment: which norm, which shared thing, which class, what happened next. In week 6 the HOWL
Tracker's weekly check-in offers the recorded station steps as starting points for the evidence
box, and the student writes what it looked like. A recorded step is not a rating.

**Pack objectives.** Each pack also tracks three objectives for the student: win the word game,
earn 20 XP across the resources, and the commitment. They are motivation, not assessment.

**The 40 minutes, in general.** Ten minutes reading, seven on words and the game, eight on the
chart, sort and quiz, eight in the Hub tool, five on frames and the memory aid, two to launch the
challenge and name the commitment. If your slot is shorter, see the trim note on each page.

## Week 1: Norms We Can Name and Keep

**HOWL: Respect.** "I am a respectful member of the King community." The behavior this week is
working cooperatively with others. The Crew decides how it wants to treat each other and writes it
in words it can check.

**The 40 minutes**

| Minutes | What happens | Resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | Read, together or alone. The reading distinguishes a rule (given to you) from a norm (chosen together) and gives the three-part test: specific, shared, revisited. | Norms We Can Name and Keep |
| 10 to 17 | Eleven words, then the Word Scramble. Crew and HOWL are defined here. | Glossary, Word Scramble |
| 17 to 25 | Chart open, sort the cards (rule, norm, or just polite), quiz. The short answer asks students to rewrite "Be a good listener" so it passes the specific test. | Rule or Norm?, Rule, Norm, or Just Polite?, quiz |
| 25 to 33 | Hub: Crew Protocols. Plan one protocol the Crew will use this week, with a way to pass. | Crew Protocols (station starts) |
| 33 to 38 | Frames: draft two norms. Memory aid: the three parts. | Sentence Frames, Remember the Three Parts |
| 38 to 40 | Launch the challenge and say the commitment aloud. | Decide Challenge: Our Crew Norms |

**In the Hub.** Crew Protocols opens on a browsable library (17 protocols by purpose). Students
pick one for this week and note it in My Crew plan. The About tab has the "New to Crew or HOWLs?"
primer and sources if anyone asks what any of this is.

**The commitment.** Notice one moment in any class where you kept a Crew norm without being
reminded. Bring it back to Crew.

**What counts as evidence.** "In science on Tuesday we were building the circuit and I waited
until Malik finished before I said the wire was in the wrong place. That is our norm about
finishing." A norm named, a class named, a moment described. Not evidence: "I was respectful all
week."

**Watch for.** The Crew writes rules ("Be respectful") and calls them norms; send them back to the
three-part test. Someone asks whether passing is a way out of Crew; the FAQ answers it (passing
keeps you in the circle, it is not leaving it). The challenge asks the Crew to vote on four norms;
if you run out of time, the vote is the opener next week.

**If you only have 15 minutes.** Reading (or the chart instead), the Hub tool, the commitment.

## Week 2: Repair When Something Goes Wrong

**HOWL: Respect.** The behavior is stewarding community resources and materials. Taking care of
what belongs to everyone, and repairing rather than punishing when something goes wrong.

**The 40 minutes**

| Minutes | What happens | Resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | Read. Stewardship, then the three repair moves: name it, own it, fix it, and why repair asks a different question than punishment. | Repair When Something Goes Wrong |
| 10 to 17 | Twelve words, then Matching. | Glossary, Matching |
| 17 to 25 | Chart, sort (which move is it, or not repair at all), quiz. Question 3 is about the person who was affected. | Punish or Repair?, Which Move Is It?, quiz |
| 25 to 33 | Hub: Restorative Circle with a fictional case. Perspective Lens on the same case if there is time. | Restorative Circle (station starts), Perspective Lens |
| 33 to 38 | Frames: script a repair in six lines. Memory aid. | Sentence Frames, Remember Name, Own, Fix |
| 38 to 40 | Launch the challenge; say the commitment. | Propose Challenge: A Stewardship Routine for Our Crew Room |

**In the Hub.** Restorative Circle asks students to set circle agreements first, then walk the
four questions. Use a fictional case. The tool's own header says it: never require public
disclosure, and do not use a circle for active safety risk. Perspective Lens takes the same case
from the other side.

**The commitment.** Steward one shared thing in any class (return it, plug it in, put it back)
without anyone asking.

**What counts as evidence.** "I plugged in the class laptop cart on Wednesday before anyone said
to." The thing, the day, unprompted. Not evidence: "I was helpful."

**Watch for.** "Does repair mean nobody gets consequences?" is the first question every year; the
FAQ says consequences and repair are not opposites. A student uses a real conflict as the circle
case; steer back to fiction, that is what the pack's cases are for. The challenge proposes an
end-of-Crew routine for the room; it works best if you actually adopt one.

**If you only have 15 minutes.** Chart, the Hub circle on the fictional case, the commitment.

## Week 3: Where Am I Right Now?

**HOWL: Responsibility.** "I take responsibility for my success as a learner." The behavior is
participating fully and mindfully, which starts with knowing your own state.

**The 40 minutes**

| Minutes | What happens | Resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | Read. Four zones, one window, and the idea that naming a state is not the same as excusing a behavior. | Where Am I Right Now? |
| 10 to 17 | Twelve words, then the Word Scramble. | Glossary, Word Scramble |
| 17 to 25 | Chart, sort (which zone), quiz. Question 4 is about what naming your state does not mean. | Four Zones, One Window, Which Zone?, quiz |
| 25 to 33 | Hub: Emotion Zones, the one-word check-in. Then Window of Tolerance for one move back. Sensory Regulation for a desk-sized move if there is time. | Emotion Zones (station starts), Window of Tolerance, Sensory Regulation |
| 33 to 38 | Frames: write your own check-in and reset. Memory aid. | Sentence Frames, Remember Zones and the Window |
| 38 to 40 | Launch the challenge; say the commitment. | Investigate Challenge: What Moves Me Back? |

**In the Hub.** Emotion Zones opens on Check-In. The nine main tabs are visible; fourteen more sit
behind More. Students do not need any of them this week. Window of Tolerance asks students to add
signs to each of three zones and then place today. The station's five active minutes are counted in
Emotion Zones and shown at the top of the screen while it is open.

**The commitment.** Do a silent one-word check-in at the start of one class, and if you are not
green, make one move before the lesson starts.

**What counts as evidence.** "Before math on Thursday I was yellow. I did the long exhale twice
and started the warm-up." A zone, a move, a class. Not evidence: "I stayed calm."

**Watch for.** "Do I have to tell the Crew why I am in yellow?" No, and the FAQ says so; the
check-in is a word, not a story. "Is it bad to be in blue a lot?" It is information, not a
verdict; if it is every day, that is a conversation with an adult, not a Crew activity. The
challenge runs for a week and asks for the pattern, not the feelings.

**If you only have 15 minutes.** Chart, the Emotion Zones check-in, the commitment.

## Week 4: One System for the Week

**HOWL: Responsibility.** The behaviors are arriving prepared and completing assignments
thoughtfully and on time. The week replaces willpower with one small system.

**The 40 minutes**

| Minutes | What happens | Resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | Read. Why willpower runs out, and the three parts of a system: capture spot, launch check, weekly reset. | One System for the Week |
| 10 to 17 | Twelve words, then Matching. | Glossary, Matching |
| 17 to 25 | Chart, sort (system or willpower), quiz. Question 2 is about why willpower fails. | Three Parts of a System, System or Willpower?, quiz |
| 25 to 33 | Hub: Executive Function, build the capture spot and launch check. Then Goal Setter for one goal the system will carry. | Executive Function (station starts), Goal Setter |
| 33 to 38 | Frames: the system in three lines. Memory aid. | Sentence Frames, Remember Capture, Launch, Reset |
| 38 to 40 | Launch the challenge; say the commitment. | Design Challenge: My One System |

**In the Hub.** Executive Function opens on Start. The header says the first step: go to Start and
pick one launch move for today, then Hold to choose the capture spot. The badge "N of 11 explored"
counts tabs visited; nothing is locked. Goal Setter wants one goal, one first step.

**The commitment.** Do the launch check at the end of every day, and count how many mornings you
had everything.

**What counts as evidence.** "I did the launch check five nights. Four mornings I had everything.
Tuesday I forgot my charger, so I moved the check to right after dinner." A count and a revision.
Not evidence: "I was more organized."

**Watch for.** "Paper or screen?" Either; the FAQ says the capture spot has to be one place, not
the best place. "I built a system and it fell apart in a week. Did I fail?" No, the part that broke
is the data; the challenge asks them to revise that part. This is the week most likely to produce a
visible change in other classes, so tell colleagues what students are trying.

**If you only have 15 minutes.** Chart, the Executive Function capture spot, the commitment.

## Week 5: Asking for What You Need

**HOWL: Perseverance.** "I persevere to produce high quality work." The behavior is advocating for
your learning by asking questions. Asking is how persevering works, not its opposite.

**The 40 minutes**

| Minutes | What happens | Resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | Read. The four moves of a clear ask: describe, express, ask, reinforce. | Asking for What You Need |
| 10 to 17 | Twelve words, then the Word Scramble. | Glossary, Word Scramble |
| 17 to 25 | Chart, sort (which move is it), quiz. Question 3 compares two ways of asking. | Four Moves of a Clear Ask, Which Move Is It?, quiz |
| 25 to 33 | Hub: DEAR MAN, build a real low-stakes ask for one class. Pair up: one asks, one plays the teacher, swap. Self-Advocacy Studio if there is time. | DEAR MAN (station starts), Self-Advocacy Studio |
| 33 to 38 | Frames: the ask in four lines. Memory aid. | Sentence Frames, Remember Describe, Express, Ask, Reinforce |
| 38 to 40 | Launch the challenge; say the commitment. | Decide Challenge: Ask It This Week |

**In the Hub.** DEAR MAN starts with two boxes: what am I asking for in one sentence, and who am I
asking. Then seven steps and a Rehearse tab. The pair rehearsal is the point of the slot; keep the
asks small and real ("could you show me one worked example of the fractions problem?").

**The commitment.** Make one specific ask in one class where you are stuck, and report back on
what happened.

**What counts as evidence.** "In ELA I asked Ms. Ortiz if she could show me one paragraph that got
a 4 on evidence. She did, and I saw mine had no quote." The ask, the class, the answer. Not
evidence: "I participated more."

**Watch for.** "Is it embarrassing to ask in front of the class?" The FAQ offers the after-class and
written options; the pack does not require a public ask. "What if the teacher says no?" Then the
next option on the list. "What if I do not know what I do not understand?" The describe move
handles it: say what you tried and where it stopped. Tell colleagues to expect some unusually
specific questions this week.

**If you only have 15 minutes.** Chart, DEAR MAN with the pair rehearsal, the commitment.

## Week 6: Feedback Is Information, Not a Verdict

**HOWL: Perseverance.** The behaviors are self-assessing with targets and rubrics, and learning
from feedback to revise. The week also closes the launch: students rate their own perseverance in
the HOWL Tracker with evidence.

**The 40 minutes**

| Minutes | What happens | Resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | Read. Verdict thinking versus map thinking, self-assess first, revise rather than redo. | Feedback Is Information, Not a Verdict |
| 10 to 17 | Twelve words, then Matching. | Glossary, Matching |
| 17 to 25 | Chart, sort (verdict or map), quiz. Question 4 is revising versus redoing. | Verdict or Map?, Verdict Thinking or Map Thinking?, quiz |
| 25 to 33 | Hub: Growth Mindset, the one-word change (Reframe It). Then HOWL Tracker, rate perseverance for the past two weeks with one piece of evidence. CBT Thought Record if "I am bad at this" is loud. | Growth Mindset (station starts), HOWL Tracker, CBT Thought Record |
| 33 to 38 | Frames: self-assess one real piece of work against its rubric. Memory aid. | Sentence Frames, Remember Gap, Map, Yet |
| 38 to 40 | Launch the challenge; say the commitment. | Explore Challenge: Rate It Before They Do |

**In the Hub.** Growth Mindset opens on Reframe It; the AI Coach tab says it is off and points
here. HOWL Tracker: on Home, switch the set to King's three HOWLs (the picker under the title) so
the grading-guide statements show. Then Weekly check-in: rate 1 to 4, add a specific example. The
check-in offers the student's recorded Crew station steps from the last three weeks as starting
points; one click drops a step into the evidence box and the student writes what it looked like.
The Home and Library tabs carry the "New to Crew or HOWLs?" primer.

**The commitment.** Rate one assignment against its rubric before turning it in, and compare your
rating to the feedback you get.

**What counts as evidence.** "I gave my lab write-up a 3 on analysis because I explained the graph
but did not say why the line bends. The feedback said the same thing." A row, a rating, a reason, a
comparison. Not evidence: "I checked my work."

**Watch for.** "What if my self-rating is higher than the teacher's?" That gap is the information;
the FAQ says so. "What if the feedback is just a grade with no comments?" Then the rubric row is the
feedback; the frames walk it. Some students will rate their perseverance a 4 on no evidence; the
check-in's rubric reveal shows what a 3 and a 4 look like in practice.

**If you only have 15 minutes.** The HOWL Tracker check-in with evidence, the commitment.

## After week 6

The station records and the HOWL Tracker check-ins are the launch's paper trail. Each is a
practice record the student owns, kept on the device and in the project file. Before the
trimester's HOWL grades, a Crew leader can ask each student to open the HOWL Tracker's History and
read one check-in aloud with its evidence. That is the conversation the grading guide's statements
were written for.

Weeks 7 to 12 exist as packs (`allopacks/crew_perspective`, `crew_upstander`, `crew_attention`,
`crew_halfway`, `crew_teamwork`, `crew_story`) and follow the same shape. Their pages in this guide
are the next writing task; until then, each pack's directions resource is the run sheet, and the
table above gives the tool and the commitment. Week 10 reopens the week-4 system and the September
HOWL Tracker goal; week 12 is the student-led conference rehearsal and closes the trimester.
