# Flea, dust mite and mouse exterior studies

Three dedicated procedural models replace the shared primitive animal forms. Each has five native selectable landmarks, cited notes, close camera views and a body ruler that excludes appendages.

- Flea: a flattened cat-flea cuticle, exposed genal and pronotal combs, segmented abdomen, long hind legs and five-part clawed feet.
- Dust mite: an oval striated cuticle, grouped mouthparts, eight jointed legs, small terminal pads and sparse long setae.
- Mouse: a continuous muzzle and crouched body, thin dished ears, directional coat, small paws, whiskers and an annulated tapering tail.

These are illustrative exteriors informed by published anatomy; they are not microscopy scans. Phone framing includes appendages, while the 2 mm flea, 0.3 mm mite and 80 mm mouse comparisons use their stated body lengths.

Validation: 64 unit checks and 12 actual WebGL browser checks passed on the applied source, including phone framing, native surface picks, notebook camera precision, 40:1 mouse/flea comparison, Liberty regressions and renderer cleanup. Both source mirrors and all four English sections match. Changes remain uncommitted.
