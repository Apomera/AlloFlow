# Aquarium simulation and visual model

Audit: 7 September 2026. Scope: the active Aquarium simulation, its shared 3D viewport, and customization. This document records the baseline state audit, the implemented simulation bridge and visual contract, and remaining model limits. It is a design and scientific review, not a claim that all acceptance checks have passed.

The 3D view should make the same saved tank easier to observe. It must preserve organism identities, habitat assignments, chemistry, and simulation time. The renderer consumes state; it does not run a second ecosystem. Materials, camera, backdrop, and presentation scale can be customized independently of biological controls, with a clear indication of which settings affect the model.

## Scientific boundary of the cues

Dissolved oxygen reflects photosynthesis, respiration, reactions, atmospheric exchange, and transport. This supports showing operating aeration together with the modeled oxygen reading, but does not justify making bubble counts represent oxygen concentration. The renderer should consume the same effective pump output used by the tick; `lastEcosystemExchange.equipment.oxygenAdded` is useful evidence from the completed interval. [USGS oxygen-budget report](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/dissolved-oxygen-chapter-6)

Observed day/night oxygen variation supports linking light and plant activity. Photosynthesis and respiration are processes within the oxygen budget; a bright leaf or diagram arrow is an explanatory representation, not an oxygen measurement. The simulation's day/night branches and coefficients remain simplified. Cosmetic blue or night tint must not silently change those coefficients. [USGS continuous diurnal monitoring study](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/new-jersey-water-science-center/science/diurnal-variations-water-quality-surface-water)

A primary zebrafish experiment found surface respiration under severe hypoxia and a decrease followed by an increase in swimming as hypoxia intensified. Development and prior acclimation changed responses. This directly argues against a universal “higher stress means faster swimming” rule or one oxygen threshold that makes every fish, invertebrate, and reptile rise to the surface. The renderer's agreed narrower approach is a modest, explicitly illustrative vitality activity factor and stress-related excursion adjustment around the existing habitat anchor. [Abdallah, Thomas and Jonz, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.116343)

White spots can occur in freshwater Ich, but similar appearances do not establish a diagnosis. The current scope therefore uses illness metadata and accessible text rather than teaching a diagnosis through textures. This review does not validate the tool's entire disease library or treatment content. [USDA research photograph](https://www.ars.usda.gov/oc/images/photos/jan18/D1937-1), [UF/IFAS authored diagnostic guide](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA006)

## Priority mismatches and implementation agreement

The audited renderer used decorative fish sizes, fixed normalized tank geometry, stress-only speed increases, and biomass scaling that rendered a full plant at zero biomass. Its scene mapping omitted hunger, vitality, disease, equipment output, and reliable feeding events. The new bridge should carry those semantics through one pure derivation step shared by the live tank and habitat studio.

The implementation adds `buildAquariumSceneDynamics` as the shared state bridge, accepted-event feeding attraction, equipment-driven aeration, model-driven algae film, normalized plant growth, and inspectable health data. `aquariumFeedingEvent` now records a unique event, sequence, tick, food type, recipient IDs, and display/hospital scope; only residents whose modeled hunger fell are accepted recipients. Live-food effects distinguish broad diet categories. The renderer does not add automatic hypoxia surfacing, disease lesions, or universal stress acceleration.

The inspector shows hunger, stress, last measured vitality with its model tick, illness records, and time in tank. Illness metadata reads the actual onset `tick`, retaining a legacy `sinceTick` fallback, and preserves individual identity. CO2 and salinity join the bridge chemistry readings. Both live 3D and illustrated views avoid full live foliage at zero biomass; the illustrated fallback retains a neutral hollow inspection marker. Unstocked decorative default plants and corals were removed. The habitat studio retains plant, object, and equipment selection. Appearance controls remain separate from simulation actions.

The audit identified a tick inconsistency: low-light plants and photosynthetic stock retained artificial minimum light factors even when equipment output was zero. The implementation now gates the daytime production branch on positive effective light output, matching the plant inspector while preserving working level-zero room light. A focused runtime suite checks this through the actual one-hour control. Zero-biomass snapshots must render honestly; subsequent biomass can increase under the existing explicit regrowth equation, which does not represent a validated recovery model.

## Acceptance and remaining scope

The enhancement should be checked against behavior, not only source text:

- Two same-species individuals preserve their own hunger, vitality, illness, names, and selection; quarantined individuals remain absent from the display scene.
- Pump absent, operating, worn, failed, repaired, and zero-condition states produce matching equipment metadata and visual effects.
- Midnight, dawn, dusk, manual lights off, a failed light, and cosmetic tint cannot contradict the declared photosynthesis state.
- One accepted feed event animates once for its recipients; refused feeding, grazing, hydration, and view toggles do not invent food.
- Plant biomass zero, missing biomass, growth, and browsing loss behave distinctly; normalized growth stays bounded and retains the plant ID.
- Admission time never becomes a newborn cue. Breeding does not create a second visual population before actual fry enter the state.
- Customization persists without changing chemistry, stocking, clock, or experimental baselines; camera and visual pause remain presentation controls.
- Keyboard inspection, reduced motion, context-loss fallback, retry, and disposal remain functional as state updates.

The focused `tests/aquarium_scene_simulation_link.test.js` suite passes all seven cases. Five execute the real one-hour control and compare failed or depleted light with switched-off baselines for low-light plants and photosynthetic stock, while preserving working room-light behavior. The sixth renders the actual live inspector, checks one ill individual among two matching species, follows the real selection callback, and confirms that illness and last-measured vitality do not transfer to the other individual. The seventh checks actual empty freshwater/reef fallback rendering and the neutral, inspectable zero-biomass plant marker. These checks also exposed and verified fixes for bridge initialization order and illness-onset field mapping. The final broader pass totals 102 passing targeted checks; renderer, component, and real-WebGL results are documented in [the visual upgrade notes](aquarium-visual-upgrade-2026-09-07.md). Existing component tests exercise the real React wrapper with a stub scene engine; they do not by themselves validate WebGL appearance, physical realism, or biological coefficients. The product remains an educational model with normalized spatial units, heuristic habitat and vitality scores, compressed breeding time, and a species/reference library that has not received exhaustive scientific review.
