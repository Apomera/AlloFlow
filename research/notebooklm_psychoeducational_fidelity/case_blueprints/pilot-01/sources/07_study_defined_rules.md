# Study-Defined SLD-Reading Simulation Rules

These rules exist only for a synthetic research benchmark. They do not determine actual eligibility under IDEA, state law, district policy, DSM criteria, or professional practice. A real eligibility decision is made by an appropriately constituted team using applicable law, policy, comprehensive evaluation data, and professional judgment.

The benchmark rules are informed by the federal principles that evaluations use multiple tools and strategies rather than a single measure, and that specific-learning-disability determinations consider inadequate achievement, progress or a relevant pattern, appropriate instruction, and alternative primary explanations. The study intentionally simplifies those principles into a controlled rule-following task.

## Permitted outcomes

- **SUPPORTED**
- **NOT SUPPORTED**
- **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**

## SUPPORTED rule

Return **SUPPORTED** only when all four conditions are supported by the supplied sources:

1. **Academic underachievement:** Multiple data sources show performance below age or grade expectations in at least one of these reading areas: basic reading, decoding unfamiliar words, reading fluency, or reading comprehension.
2. **Learning pattern:** The sources show either insufficient progress after an appropriate period of targeted, evidence-based reading intervention or a relevant pattern of academic strengths and weaknesses.
3. **Alternative-primary-explanation review:** The available sources do not indicate that the reading underachievement is primarily explained by lack of appropriate reading instruction, limited English proficiency, a visual, hearing, or motor disability, intellectual disability, emotional disturbance, cultural factors, or environmental or economic disadvantage.
4. **Educational impact and need:** The reading difficulty interferes with access to or progress in grade-level work, and the sources support a need for continued intensive, explicit reading instruction beyond ordinary undifferentiated classroom instruction.

## NOT SUPPORTED rule

Return **NOT SUPPORTED** when the sources are sufficient to evaluate all four conditions but one or more conditions is not met.

## INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE rule

Return **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** when the sources do not contain enough information to evaluate one or more required conditions. Do not fill missing evidence by assumption.

## Reporting requirements

- Identify the reading area or areas supported by the data.
- State the evidence used for each of the four conditions.
- Preserve qualifications and conflicting information.
- Do not treat one test score as sufficient.
- Describe the result as a study-defined simulation rather than an actual legal or clinical determination.

Federal background consulted in designing—but not legally incorporated into—this fictional rule includes 34 C.F.R. §§ 300.304 and 300.309.
