# Grove Journey: first playable implementation

September 4, 2026. Local implementation in `stem_lab/stem_tool_treelab.js` and its desktop public mirror. This is an introductory ecological campaign, not a calibrated forest forecast or an evolutionary genetics model.

## What is playable

The optional Grove Journey tab offers an eight-year run across nine named habitat patches. The starting grove includes a 40-year oak, a 25-year aspen and a four-year aspen, generated by the existing annual growth model under stated favourable starting conditions. Adults bring the reproductive carbon accumulated during those preceding years.

Each year presents a forecast and three resource priorities: build roots, keep reserves or invest in offspring. These are explicit carbon-allocation presets. Every individual uses its own budget; the player-level choice is a game abstraction rather than a claim of collective tree planning or unrestricted carbon sharing. Reproduction investment offers oak seeds plus local aspen shoots, or seeds from both species.

The map persists across turns. Selecting a patch reveals its inhabitants, age, reproductive origin and optional species traits and food reserves. New arrivals are distinct from descendants that survive beyond their arrival year. Success at the end of year eight requires living established descendants in at least two patches. Parent death does not automatically eliminate a surviving lineage.

Completed years leave a receipt naming the event and the priority used, food balance, attempted reproduction, carbon expenditure, arrivals and losses. A field journal records observations across runs without granting stat bonuses. Players may rewind a year, replay the same grove or return to setup. The separate single-tree lab state remains intact, and its clock pauses while viewing the grove.

## Two event modes

**Event deck** uses four authored event templates: a gentle year, rain, drought and a storm. The first year is gentle; later draws are deterministic from the grove code. This first slice prevents back-to-back dry years to give beginners recovery opportunities.

**Generated weather** combines bounded temperature, moisture and storm strength using procedural rules. The first year is also gentle. This is not an AI event generator. Later temperatures range from 18–29 °C, baseline moisture from 0.24–0.84 and storm leaf loss from 0.15–0.35 when a storm occurs. Patch modifiers and persistent storm gaps determine local conditions. These ranges and event relationships are teaching assumptions, not fitted climate distributions.

Weather, storm location, dispersal and establishment use separate keyed random draws. Changing the number of reproductive attempts cannot reroll later weather. The same code, event mode and choices reproduce the same state.

Future AI-written events should use the same validated mechanical event schema. An optional generated narrative could explain an approved event, while the engine owns host eligibility, severity, duration and biological consequences. Do not let generated prose silently introduce mechanics or change the outcome after a learner commits. Unavailable generation should fall back to the authored description. No external generation service is called by this implementation.

## Model boundaries

- One turn calls the existing `simulateYear` once for each previously living tree. There is no four-season multiplication of annual growth. The prototype uses an annual forecast and outcome rather than interactive seasonal timesteps.
- Soil moisture and light are prescribed site inputs; neighbouring trees do not draw down a common water store. Introductory storms target one of the two founding adult-canopy patches. A storm removes a fraction of leaf tissue in that patch before the annual calculation, and adds 0.18 to that patch's light setting, capped at one, for the rest of the run. A new gap requires living leaf tissue at that site; storms over an empty site cannot create extra light. This is a simplified canopy-gap model.
- Maturity gates of 20 years for oak and 10 for aspen are explicit scenario assumptions. The species' existing reproduction routes and per-attempt carbon costs apply. Attempt count is capped at three per mature tree in an offspring-priority year. Other priorities accumulate reproductive savings but do not initiate attempts.
- `seedsBanked` remains reproductive carbon, not a literal seed count. Each attempt is debited once. Establishment combines the existing strategy probability with bounded moisture and light suitability and available patch capacity. Successful arrivals begin at age one after the year's existing-tree update.
- Local aspen shoots share a recorded clone-group identity and disperse only to the same or cardinally adjacent patch. This version does not simulate root-network resource exchange or genetic variation within a species. Seed offspring identities do not imply measured genetic independence or automatic disease resistance.
- There can be at most three living trees in each of nine patches. Capacity is a teaching constraint, not a real planting-density recommendation. The map uses SVG symbols, not a detailed 3D canopy per tree; symbols are explicitly not to scale. Dead individuals retain lineage records.
- Saved campaign state is versioned seed, event mode and at most eight choices. Canonical population state is reconstructed by deterministic replay. Campaign state and journal fields are separate from the lab tree and experiments.

## Trait cards, evolution and GMO experiments

The current selectable cards change allocation, not genes. Existing species traits can be inspected. The science disclosure explicitly distinguishes these resource decisions from natural selection, breeding and gene modification.

An evolution extension should begin with existing, heritable variation within a species and record parentage, offspring traits and population frequencies. Environmental pressures affect survival and reproduction; variants do not appear because organisms need them. Compare multiple runs to distinguish selection from chance. Changes must occur on appropriately labelled generation timescales, not by turning an adult oak into a newly adapted genotype after one dry summer. These distinctions follow [UC Berkeley's explanation of natural selection](https://evolution.berkeley.edu/evolution-101/mechanisms-the-processes-of-evolution/natural-selection/).

Selecting which parents reproduce is artificial selection. Deliberately changing a gene is genetic engineering. A future optional trait experiment could compare a baseline and a hypothetical modified line under identical events, with explicit costs, uncertain effects and inheritance assumptions. Do not present a generic “drought resistance +20%” slider as an established effect of a named gene. A trait-level model should identify itself as hypothetical unless supported by a defensible genotype-to-phenotype model.

Avoid automatic adaptive mutation cards, guaranteed resistance, permanent biological upgrades from winning runs, and the implication that every trait is controlled by one gene. An evolution investigation, a breeding experiment and a GMO experiment should have distinct roles and labels.

## Invasive species and old growth

Emerald ash borer fits an ash-focused scenario with researched host susceptibility and disturbance dynamics. The current oak-and-aspen grove does not simulate it. Different invasive organisms need distinct mechanisms, such as damage to a susceptible host or altered establishment conditions. Increasing species count must not confer universal immunity. See the [Forest Service's ash-borer ecosystem research](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/34553).

An old-growth chapter should start with an existing forest and ask learners to sustain regeneration and structural habitat through disturbance. It would need large living trees, younger recruitment, snags, downed wood and canopy gaps, with criteria appropriate to its forest type. The Forest Service identifies large living trees, large snags and large logs as important structural components in its [Douglas-fir old-growth account](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/5546); this should not be treated as a universal numeric definition for all forests.

Neither invasive-species play, evolutionary genetics, GMO intervention nor old-growth dynamics is implemented in this first slice. The in-app disclosure identifies these boundaries without presenting inactive upgrade buttons.

## Campaign 3D close-up

The campaign now includes an optional 3D close-up of a single selected tree, added after the first playable slice. A **Habitat map / 3D close-up** switch sits above the map; the 2D map remains the default and is the only whole-grove view. Selecting a patch and choosing its 3D action, or switching to the close-up and picking a patch and tree, renders that campaign individual with the existing single-tree 3D scene.

What is shown is the real campaign state, not a stock illustration. `groveSceneState(state, patch, requestedId)` picks the requested individual within the patch, otherwise a living tree, then a dead one, and returns null for an empty patch so the interface can say that no tree has arrived. Conditions come from the last completed event, or the starting patch conditions in year zero, never from the next forecast, and the derivation does not mutate the run. The view is labelled as a summer illustration of the tree's current state. Roots are a schematic cutaway because the model tracks root mass rather than root architecture. Background woodland is scenery; the habitat map records the actual grove. Viewing a tree does not advance time.

Controls include patch and tree selectors, camera rotate, tilt, zoom and reset buttons, and leaves/trunk/roots inspection with short explanations tied to the campaign tree. The viewer is the same module-level scene as the lab tree, reused through a stable attach point and subject key so one canvas survives growth and tree changes and is released when the map is shown or an empty patch is chosen. Lab tree, experiments and the lab clock stay independent; returning to Grow restores the lab tree. When 3D rendering fails, the map, measurements and campaign controls stay usable and the camera buttons are disabled. Theme tokens, high contrast, reduced motion and phone layouts are supported.

This does not add a 3D whole-forest view, fullscreen, genetics, invasive species or old-growth dynamics; the boundaries above still apply.

## Evidence, prediction and replay comparison

After the 3D close-up, a further pass made the campaign's consequences visible and gave learners a way to test their own expectations.

**Young-tree mortality.** Campaign worlds previously recorded no deaths at all across 48 sampled seeds. A stated scenario rule now gives trees three years old or younger an extra chance of dying when their patch is dry that year: risk rises linearly as patch soil moisture falls below 0.35, is capped at 80 percent, and is halved at full species drought tolerance. Each draw is keyed on the year and the individual, so it does not disturb the weather sequence and a code still replays identically. Adults continue to die only through the existing carbon-starvation rule, which the disclosure now says explicitly. This is a teaching assumption, not a calibrated seedling survival curve.

**Landing and loss records.** Receipts now carry `landings` (patch, route, parent, outcome and limiting condition) and `losses` (id, patch, species, age, cause). The limiting condition names whichever prescribed factor most reduced the establishment odds; a miss at full suitability is reported as chance alone, which keeps the route's base establishment probability honest. The interface reads these into a "Where it happened" list, snag glyphs and arrival/loss badges on the map, and richer patch labels for assistive technology.

**Predict, then check.** An optional pre-year prediction (expected arrivals; surplus or shortfall) is stored beside the run as learner data and compared with the evidence afterwards. Misses are explained from the year's conditions: dry soil closing stomata, lost leaf tissue, or the ongoing maintenance cost of living wood. Predictions are removed on rewind and cleared on replay or new setup so they never describe a year that no longer exists.

**Run ledger.** Completed runs are remembered (most recent twelve) and the ending compares runs that share a code and event mode. Because those runs share weather, differences come from the priority sequence, the routes chosen and each landing's keyed chance draw; the note says so.

## Stored food, grouped evidence, discoveries and the journey chart

Receipts now carry `reserves` and `banked`, the sums of non-negative stored food and reproductive savings across living trees after the year. The evidence card shows both with the year-on-year change, so the effect of Keep reserves is observable rather than implied. Grouped "Where it happened" lines merge patches with identical landing outcomes. `groveDiscoveries` adds `young-loss` when any recorded loss was a dry-soil death; the interface adds `prediction-hit` and `fair-replay` when a saved year matched a prediction or a second completed run of the same code and mode enters the ledger. The field journal's line chart plots living trees and established descendants by year from receipts, with year zero taken from the founders; it uses validated colour pairs, dash and marker shape as secondary encoding, per-point hover titles and an aria-label listing every value, and the timeline list remains the table view. None of this changes the simulation or the save format.

## Forecast risk preview and reflection notes

The interface derives `dryNext` (patches whose next-year prescribed moisture is below 0.35) and `youngAtRisk` (living trees that will be three or younger next year standing in those patches) from the same `groveEnvironment` call the engine uses, so the preview cannot disagree with the mortality rule. Ledger entries may carry a `note` (240 characters) typed on the ending card; it is shown in later comparisons of the same code and mode. Simulation and save format are unchanged.
