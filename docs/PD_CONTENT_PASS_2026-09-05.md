# PD library content quality pass (2026-09-05)

Scope: the 8 approved modules in `catalog/pd/approved/`. Read in full, checked for accuracy of
cited frameworks and research, pedagogical soundness, quiz construction, and structural defects
(branching graph, gates, thresholds).

## What was accurate and left alone

- Retrieval practice: Roediger & Karpicke (2006) and the testing-effect framing are correct;
  low-stakes and spacing guidance matches the literature.
- Actionable feedback: Hattie & Timperley (2007) feed up / back / forward and the Wiliam rule of
  thumb are correctly attributed.
- Academic vocabulary: the Beck, McKeown & Kucan three-tier framework and student-friendly
  definitions are correctly described.
- De-escalation: the Colvin & Sugai acting-out cycle phases are correct; the module carries the two
  cautions that matter (function-based support prevents the next cycle; follow the school's crisis
  plan). The branching graph has every node reachable and both endings reachable; the two bad
  choices lead to realistic consequences rather than a lecture.
- Family conversations: practice guidance without overclaiming; the persona rubric asks for
  connection, plain language, family expertise, acknowledged emotion and one next step each.
- Learning styles are handled as the memory guidance asks: named as not supported by research,
  with multiple representations for all learners as the alternative.

## Defects found and fixed

1. **Guideline names were from UDL Guidelines 2.2.** CAST released Guidelines 3.0 in 2024 and
   renamed Comprehension to Building Knowledge (3), Physical Action to Interaction (4), Executive
   Functions to Strategy Development (6), Recruiting Interest to Welcoming Interests & Identities (7)
   and Self-Regulation to Emotional Capacity (9). All three UDL modules now use the 3.0 names with
   the 2.2 names noted for teachers who learned them, link to the 3.0 principle pages, and say why
   CAST renamed Guideline 9. The brain-network framing stays, attributed to CAST rather than stated
   as neuroscience fact, since CAST retains it in 3.0.
2. **Longest-answer tell.** 17 of the 19 quiz questions had the correct option as the uniquely
   longest one, a test-wiseness cue that survives the runtime answer shuffle. Distractors were
   rewritten by hand to matched specificity (never padded); keys and answer indices are untouched.
   After the pass: 0 of 19 uniquely longest, 0 of 19 uniquely shortest.
3. **A throwaway distractor.** "none of these" in the first Representation question is now a
   plausible fourth option.
4. **Retrieval practice omitted feedback.** The primer and the checklist now say to show answers
   soon after each retrieval attempt, which the literature treats as part of the effect.

## Second round (same day)

- **New module: Neuromyths in Education: What We Got Wrong** (`neuromyths-what-we-got-wrong`,
  1.0.0, order 9). Learning-styles matching (Pashler et al., 2008), left/right-brain dominance
  (Nielsen et al., 2013), the 10% claim, the Mozart effect (Pietschnig et al., 2010 meta-analysis),
  with multiple intelligences and growth mindset handled as "careful wording, not dismissal"
  (Yeager et al., 2019; Sisk et al., 2018), and the replicated practices to use instead. Prevalence
  framing from Dekker et al. (2012). Quiz built without the length tell from the start
  (0 of 4 uniquely longest or shortest after one correction).
- **Persona scaffold.** The family-conversation scenario now ends with a model first line and the
  instruction not to read the data first, for learners who freeze at turn one. Module 1.1.0.
