# Pass fourteen model work: cuttlefish camouflage surface

## Read-only diagnosis

The pass-thirteen common and mimic octopus captures show similarly tan, round mantles with fine dark stipple. The pass-eleven cuttlefish capture has a stronger, species-specific silhouette after the fin and eye work, but its broad dorsal mantle still reads as one glossy tan surface with uniformly printed speckles. The pass-six Humboldt capture already has a dedicated red/pale surface branch. The most bounded next improvement is therefore a distinct cuttlefish skin treatment, preserving its established geometry and leaving Humboldt and the other ten species alone.

Relevant production anchors are `createCLHuntAnimal` near line 11317, the shared skin compiler near 11327, the existing Humboldt surface branch near 11334, the arm material clone near 11345, the fin material clone near 11533, and the pattern/display uniforms in `update` near 11594. Current cuttle skin uses mesh-local `position`: the mantle, head, arms, eyelids and fins do not share the same origin or geometric scale. Any anatomical dorsal mask or broad mantle patch added directly to that coordinate would appear in inconsistent places across those surfaces. The uniform tan color is also partly intentional: the dive loop copies the current camouflage color into the material near line 14839. A surface upgrade must retain that input and the game's camouflage scoring rather than assigning a permanently dramatic palette.

## Proposed bounded change

Add a cuttlefish-only skin compiler that uses an animal-relative coordinate frame. Replace the generic high-frequency stipple with lower-contrast fine pigment variation beneath small and medium warm-brown/cream mottling. A softened central dorsal light patch and paired lateral mantle patches give the broad mantle an organized pattern at useful viewing distances. Keep those larger components subdued on sand and increase their expression using the existing `clPattern` substrate input on grass and rock. A restrained paler ventral field and slight material roughness variation improve surface readability without displacing vertices or claiming to simulate papillae.

Retain the existing passing-cloud term and its `clDisplay`/`clPhase` behavior. Keep body-color tinting, camouflage strength, detection, movement, feeding, eyes, fin geometry, inspection and reduced-motion contracts unchanged. All iris and pupil shaders stay byte-identical. Skin-colored sockets and lids naturally inherit the same cuttle skin material. No geometry, textures, meshes, random calls, emissive display or new autonomous animation is proposed.

The animal-relative matrix and a distinct cuttle skin cache identity are shared consistently by the skin, arm and fin material clones. The Humboldt compiler and cache identity remain unchanged. The matrix follows the existing root transform update pattern, uses a reusable `Matrix4`, and does not allocate each frame. Pattern masks are broad and continuous rather than increasing speckle frequency. The original mesh-local `clSkinPos` varying remains intact for the exact existing passing-cloud sine and strength term; the separate `clCuttlePos` varying supplies the coherent anatomical pattern. Replacing the old varying globally would have shifted the passing-cloud phase differently across meshes.

## Biological basis and limits

[Chiao et al. (2010), Mottle camouflage patterns in cuttlefish](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/213/2/187/9930/Mottle-camouflage-patterns-in-cuttlefish), reports experimentally elicited patterns in *Sepia officinalis*. Its Figure 2 describes small and medium pigment aggregations, and four paired mantle components surrounding a central light component. The study also distinguishes quiet uniform/stipple appearances from stronger mottle and disruptive patterns and relates their expression to background properties. This supports organized, differently scaled patches rather than an identical noise field over every surface. The proposed patch placement, contrast, palette and roughness remain illustrative approximations; the simulator's three substrate categories do not model an animal's visual assessment of a real scene. The central patch should not be represented as a permanent white marking, nor should the shader imply full neural pattern control or raised skin texture.

## Cost and verification plan

The current extracted cuttlefish rig contains 27 meshes, 27 owned geometries, 10 materials, 6,232 geometry vertices, and 32,028 submitted triangles including sucker instances. The material change adds zero meshes, vertices, triangles, textures or draw calls. The replacement keeps the existing budget of three three-dimensional noise samples, or 24 lattice hashes per fragment, and adds simple anatomical masks and bounded roughness arithmetic. A cuttle-only bounded lattice hash replaces the old large sine hash to avoid amplifying precision-sensitive printed speckles. The new resource cost is one reusable matrix uniform, one root-frame update, and one extra vec3 varying (three interpolated floats). Actual shader and GPU cost must be checked after integration rather than inferred from triangle count.

The standalone `apply-cuttle-surface.cjs` script changes four exact source blocks inside the factory. Its `--check` audit passed module syntax, 48 complete geometry/pose comparisons across all twelve species and four update states, and 44 complete material/shader/uniform comparisons across the eleven unaffected species. It also confirmed the four cuttle skin materials share one frame uniform and that cuttle iris and pupil shaders remain exact. No source outside the factory is replaced, including the twelve existing screen-reader wrappers.

Material names are `cl-cuttle-skin-material`, `cl-cuttle-arm-material` and `cl-cuttle-fin-material` (two separately owned fin materials). All four use cache key `cl-cuttle-surface-v14` and shared uniform `clCuttleSurfaceFrame`. The compiler's actual r128 `(shader, renderer)` callback selects filtered medium/fine detail only on WebGL2 or a WebGL1 renderer reporting `OES_standard_derivatives`. The bundled physical/standard program already requests the corresponding extension in its prefix; the callback does not mutate late extension parameters. Unsupported or absent renderer capability uses fixed medium weight 0.45 and fine weight zero, with no cuttle derivative calls. The same anatomical color structure remains available in that fallback.

`tests/cephalopodlab_cuttle_surface.test.js` contains six focused cases for the actual bundled compiler and all clones, derivative/fallback paths, production scalar GLSL bounds and anatomy masks, coherent root coordinates through translated/rotated/nonuniformly scaled parents, shared uniforms and frozen resource identities, and the pre-change all-species geometry/eleven-species shader fingerprints. The first integrated run exposed an over-strict test assumption that the bounded hash would yield more than 100 distinct lattice levels. Its final 127-state mixer actually has 75 attainable levels, all reached by the sample grid. That assertion was replaced with exact attainable-support coverage, near-full-range checks, and three short-spatial-repeat checks; no production adjustment was made. Root subsequently reported all six focused cases and both native low/balanced living-surface browser cases passing. Those browser checks include compiled programs, shared uniforms, display controls, pause/reduced-motion behavior and disposal. No browser was launched and no canonical source or mirrors were edited by this agent.

## Strong-pattern capture fixture

The guarded `add-rock-capture.cjs` patch adds a separate rock-camouflage view to the parent-owned `visual-review.cjs`. Its check-only run passed four exact helper guards, the ignore-rule guard and syntax. It leaves the existing sand, Humboldt and coral capture blocks intact. `--rock-only` skips those existing views, allowing `--initial --rock-only` against the saved baseline and then `--rock-only` against canonical source. The new images are `initial-cuttlefish-rock-dorsal.png` and `cuttlefish-rock-dorsal.png`.

The fixture installs a counted clock before the harness's normal `__mount`, so initial tint and body phase begin from the same simulation time. It selects an actual nearby rock with a straight collision-clear route and an endpoint strictly between the live cuttlefish collision radius (`0.62 * rock radius + 0.22`) and the substrate-detection radius. It mirrors coral/grass precedence when choosing that endpoint. Navigation uses actual keyboard A/D/W events: A increases yaw, D decreases yaw at two radians per second, and W follows the animal's sine/cosine forward direction. Positive clock steps are only 0.025 or 0.05 seconds; other rendered frames have zero delta. No player, environment, material, lighting, visibility or shader uniform is assigned by the fixture.

After reaching the endpoint, 60 counted 0.05-second stationary steps allow the normal camouflage color transition to settle. The helper verifies that the live shared `clPattern` is 0.9 and that the player remains inside the selected rock's valid collision/detection annulus. Normal F, Orbit right, and three Higher inputs produce the dorsal view. `cuttle-rock-fixture.json` records rock identity/radius, waypoint, the actual key/step sequence, final root position/quaternion, material tint, pattern, phase and counted time. The final run must match the baseline fixture exactly. This is a test scenario through normal gameplay state, not an override of the camouflage shader. Route availability and before/after equality are intentionally runtime guards; the patch-script check does not claim a browser capture has completed. Root applies and executes it after the current capture process ends.
