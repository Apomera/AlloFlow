# T. rex, giraffe and elephant anatomy studies

Dedicated procedural models replace three generic animal forms. Each model has five selectable surfaces, cited notes and close camera views. The measurement references remain fixed as users inspect, record observations or compare animals.

- T. rex: sculpted head and limbs, curved teeth, surface scales and a jaw that opens for inspection. The whole view closes the mouth.
- Giraffe: a reticulated coat with consistent texture scale, shaped ears and ossicones, and split hooves.
- Elephant: thin contoured ears, a tapered trunk, curved tusks and padded feet. The ruler measures shoulder height.

These are composed exterior illustrations. Soft tissue, colour and individual markings are interpreted. The scenes use restrained lighting, a floor and contact shadows; phone framing includes the full silhouette.

Validation: 72 unit checks and 15 actual WebGL browser checks passed on the applied source. Checks cover 15 native landmark picks, all-vertex phone framing, jaw pose, fixed references, notebook precision, comparisons, earlier models and renderer cleanup. Both source mirrors and all four English sections match. Changes remain uncommitted.
