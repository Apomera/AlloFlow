# Machine Lab: ninth visual pass

The lever and inclined-plane stations now have more complete mechanical assemblies and clearer force motion.

- **Lever:** a bolted bearing cradle supports the axle, with braced uprights, bearing rings, collars and a marked effort pad. The load sits on a metal seat on the beam. Downward effort now raises the load; vertical force arrows track their beam contact points. Travel limits keep the beam above the bench at extreme arm ratios, and the camera includes the longer ends.
- **Inclined plane:** deck seams, underside ribs, support feet, hinge pins and diagonal braces make its construction visible. Support heights follow the actual incline. Shallow ramps keep their ribs above the bench and use short supports; near-vertical ramps avoid overlapping pairs of posts.
- **Learning cues:** the lever observation changes for long, short and equal effort arms. The distance and force readouts use the same precision as the mechanical-advantage badge.

**754/754 tests passed across 25 Machine Lab test files.** Thirteen added checks cover lever load contact, effort/load direction, force-arrow attachment, reset, reduced motion, beam clearance, camera extents, ramp support attachment, rib clearance and ratio-dependent captions. Syntax and whitespace checks passed; source and desktop copies are byte-identical.

Chromium with real WebGL rendered ready and moving machines, both extreme lever ratios, shallow and near-vertical ramps, and 320px/390px mobile layouts. Light, dark and high-contrast runs reported no page errors or horizontal overflow; mobile status cards and legends remained outside the canvas. A final high-contrast capture verifies the corrected short-arm explanation and matching 0.05 ratio readouts.

These changes are local. Deployment remains paused while shared work continues.
