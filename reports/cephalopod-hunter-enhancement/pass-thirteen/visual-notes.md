# Pass thirteen: benthic octopus eye surfaces

The remaining six benthic octopus models still use the original flattened gold iris spheres, blue-looking pupil material and separate white highlight dots. These layers read as buttons in the pass-twelve common-octopus reef and plant-detail captures, while the four swimming models already use curved darker eye surfaces. This pass addresses that specific inconsistency.

## Scope and anatomy limits

`apply-model-eyes.cjs` inserts an explicit branch for `commonOcto`, `blueRinged`, `mimicOcto`, `giantPacific`, `caribReef` and `coconutOcto`. It retains the existing horizontal aperture silhouette, replacing the layered eye finish with a shallow curved iris, dark pupil dome, recessed skin socket and upper skin fold. The socket center remains at X ±0.310, Y 0.075, Z 0.410 times animal scale. Iris and pupil YZ centers remain 0.075 and 0.430 times scale. There is no whole-eye translation or change to head/body geometry.

[Soto, Kelber and Hanke (2020), The Pupillary Response of the Common Octopus](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2020.01112/full), is original research documenting the common octopus's pupillary response and horizontal aperture under brighter conditions. The model retains a fixed illustrative light-adapted opening; it does not simulate dilation, independent eye control, or counter-rotation that keeps a living octopus's pupil level. Extending the visual treatment to the other five models preserves their existing aperture category rather than asserting species-specific measurements. The [Monterey Bay Aquarium's giant Pacific octopus exhibit photographs](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/visit/exhibits/giant-pacific-octopus) provide an additional direct animal reference for an eye integrated into the surrounding skin.

The shared warm neutral iris palette, subtle radial fibers and restrained water reflection are illustrative material choices, not measured species-specific eye colors. No reference imagery is bundled. Humboldt squid, nautilus and all four previously upgraded swimmers retain their complete rigs. Every non-eye mesh, skin shader, body pose, feeding behavior and existing screen-reader wrapper remains outside this patch.

## Rendering and resource cost

Each eye pair keeps eight meshes/draw calls. Geometry decreases from 3,400 vertices and 5,760 indexed triangles to 2,040 vertices and 3,408 triangles: 1,360 fewer vertices and 2,352 fewer triangles for each affected animal. Two shared iris/pupil materials replace the prior iris/pupil and two separate highlight materials. Sockets and lids reuse the animal's existing skin material. All eye buffers remain static; there are no textures, new random draws or per-frame eye allocations.

Changed mesh names are `cl-eye-rim`, `cl-iris`, `cl-pupil` and `cl-eye-lid`. The previous `cl-eye-highlight` name remains as a compatibility group containing one lid per side. No new eye `userData` contract is introduced. Material names are `cl-octopus-iris-material` and `cl-octopus-pupil-material`. The opaque physical pupil uses a correctly linearized dark color and the distinct cache key `cl-octopus-pupil-water-v13`; its bundled shader marker is `vec3 clOctoEyeN=`. The reflection responds to view direction only, without a clock, emissive flash or animated glint.

## Giant Pacific grounding follow-up

The ordinary `giantPacific-eyes.png` capture exposed a separate pre-existing placement problem: the giant's body and arms use scale 1.4, while its player root rested at the same 0.55 m floor offset as the unit-scale octopuses. Mid-arm sections sank into the seabed and the curled tips reappeared as apparently detached pieces. The eye patch did not alter these arms, sucker instances, body poses or placement rules; the protected non-eye fingerprints remained unchanged.

`apply-giant-grounding.cjs` adds a local rest height immediately after the existing body-scale declaration. Only `giantPacific` uses `FLOOR_REST_Y * bodyScale`, giving 0.77 m. The global constant and all other species remain at 0.55 m. Seven exact replacement guards cover this declaration and the six existing spawn, initial-altitude, fallback-altitude, terrain-clamp, propulsion-floor and substrate-distance sites. Keeping the last two references consistent prevents the corrected resting animal from incorrectly counting as hovering above camouflage or propulsion contact. The script verifies that reversing those replacements restores the entire source and that the complete animal factory remains byte-identical. It adds no geometry, material, animation, RNG, draw-call or per-frame allocation cost.

The in-memory candidate audit passed all guards and syntax checks. It transformed every visible body-mesh vertex and sucker-instance vertex through the actual rig matrices at 14 animation times, four yaw angles, and both idle and walking states: 112 poses and 2,036,608 rendered vertex samples. Against the production terrain-height function at the reef origin, the baseline's minimum clearance was −0.202740 m and the candidate's was +0.017260 m, including the existing vertical bob. `tests/cephalopodlab_giant_grounding.test.js` adds three cases covering this sampled clearance, giant-only initialization, and the production altitude clamp/floor references through resting, ascent, descent and depth limits. These checks preserve rather than rewrite swimming, rock collision, evasion or body poses.

This correction is a scale-aware resting offset, not terrain-following arm animation. The CPU clearance claim is limited to the sampled poses at the reef origin against the terrain-height function; it does not establish clearance on every steep shelf slope or around rocks. Root owns the complementary ordinary-inspection check against the rendered terrain triangles, integration, mirror synchronization and final GPU verification.
