Principles & design rationale

How AlloFlow is designed

AlloFlow does the preparation so teachers can do the teaching.

Differentiating one lesson five ways by hand, late at night, is the work that burns teachers out and pulls their attention away from students. AlloFlow takes on that labor: leveled companions, glossaries, practice, checks, accessible copies. The part only a person can do stays with the teacher: noticing a student, knowing them, deciding what they need next.

AI in a classroom can quietly move a student's attention, and their relationships, onto a screen. We think the opposite is possible. A tool brings more humanity into a room when it does the work that crowds people out. The principles below are how we hold AlloFlow to that.

Our principles

Three tests and five commitments

We judge every feature against three tests. Five commitments keep those tests honest.

Three tests for every feature

  1. 1

    Time

    It gives a teacher minutes back with a student. A feature should remove preparation work, not add screen management. If it needs more teacher clicks than the task it replaces, it fails.

  2. 2

    Handoff

    It ends by sending the student back to people. A good activity finishes with a conversation, a partner, a group discussion, or a question for the teacher. A student should not finish a lesson having talked only to software.

  3. 3

    Seeing

    It helps the teacher understand the student, not just score them. Evidence should show how a student thinks, where they got stuck, and what helped. Automated numbers are labeled as estimates until a person reviews them.

Where we are today: Time and Seeing shape what ships now; building clear human handoffs into every activity is ongoing work.

Five commitments

  1. 1

    The teacher is the author.

    AI writes drafts. A teacher reviews them before students see them, and the teacher's judgment is final.

  2. 2

    The original stays.

    Adapted readings, glossaries, and other supports sit beside the original text. They do not replace it.

  3. 3

    Collect less, forget by default.

    No student accounts. Codenames instead of names. Live classroom messages expire on their own.

  4. 4

    Work without AI when it can.

    Practice, checks, and many activities run with no AI and no account, including offline.

  5. 5

    Say only what is true.

    AlloFlow is open source under AGPL v3. We describe what it does today and say plainly what we have not measured or verified.

Why AlloFlow is built this way

Teachers generate first, then share

The teacher uses AI while preparing. Students receive the reviewed result, not a live AI.

Why we chose it

  • A person checks what students see. AI makes mistakes and carries bias, and the students least able to catch an error are often the ones a differentiated resource is for. Preparing first gives the teacher a chance to read, fix, and approve every resource before it reaches a student.
  • Every student gets the version the teacher approved. When each student generates their own content, thirty students get thirty unreviewed versions. When the teacher prepares once, the class gets one reviewed set, differentiated on purpose.
  • Less computing for the same lesson. One generation, reviewed and reused by a whole class, takes far fewer AI requests than every student generating their own quizzes, readings, and images. Fewer requests mean less computing and less energy. We have not measured AlloFlow's energy use, so we do not publish a figure.
  • Students need no AI account. A shared lesson runs on a student's device without a provider key, sign-in, or connection to an AI service.

What AlloFlow does today

  • Lessons shared through QR homework links, the Class Mailbox, live sessions, and lesson packs open with student AI turned off. If the setting is missing or unclear, AI stays off. A student cannot turn it on by editing the link.
  • AI image generation is blocked for students when student AI is off.
  • A teacher can let students connect their own AI provider only after confirming that every student in the project is 18 or older, for adult education and college classes.
  • A Hide student AI tools switch removes student-facing AI from a project while the teacher keeps their authoring tools.
  • The spoken sounds in Quick Practice & Checks were generated once, ahead of time. Students hear recordings; nothing is generated while they practice.

When students use AI directly

Some activities are built for a student to talk with AI: the Socratic tutor, and the story and images in Adventure Mode. They reach a student only where an adult has already chosen to run AlloFlow with AI:

  • A district that has enabled Gemini for student accounts and permits AlloFlow in Gemini Canvas.
  • A school or teacher that set up a local AI model on a device. In a student role, AlloFlow ignores any AI key saved on that device and uses only the local model.
  • A parent or homeschooling family running AlloFlow in Gemini Canvas with their own Google account.

The adult in charge decides. The tutor is on by default in these places because that adult has already chosen AI for their students. They can turn off the tutor, or all student AI, in the project's settings, and can lock Adventure settings. Every shared link keeps student AI off.

Architectural choices

How those principles show up in the software

Each design boundary below is built into the workflows rather than left as a reminder on a poster.

The original text stays

An adapted reading is a companion to the original, not a replacement. AlloFlow marks adapted text as supplemental by default and lets students view the original, the adapted version, or both.

Simplifying a text can quietly remove the idea the lesson is about. Keeping the original in view keeps every student connected to the same shared text and the same class discussion.

Practice that runs without AI

Quick Practice & Checks makes offline practice files and checks in the browser—word sounds, math facts, vocabulary, and more. Core makers use no AI, no account, and no uploads, and student practice files work completely offline (two optional teacher voice pages run a local voice model on your device, and lookup helpers connect only when asked).

Many classroom needs do not require AI, and a tool that works without it is cheaper, more private, and more dependable.

No student accounts, and live data that expires

Students join with generated codenames instead of names or accounts. When a teacher uses the Class Mailbox for live lessons, it runs on the teacher's own Google account, not AlloFlow servers. Its live messages expire within 45 minutes and session markers within 6 hours. Schools can also run live lessons on their own Firebase deployment or local Desktop setup.

In a student role, AlloFlow does not use an AI key saved on that device. Data a tool never collects cannot leak, be sold, or be misused. See the privacy and responsible AI chapter for full data-path details.

Your provider, your hardware

AlloFlow runs in Gemini Canvas, in a browser with a provider of your choice, or on AlloFlow Desktop with local models. A school can also run its own server.

Schools differ in budgets, contracts, and privacy rules, so AlloFlow lets them choose where AI runs rather than choosing for them. Compare access paths →

Numbers are drafts until a person reviews them

Automated reading-fluency estimates keep the original estimate alongside the teacher's corrections. In the Report Writer, extracted scores start unverified and lock only when a reviewer verifies them.

Our guidance is direct: no AI output, dashboard pattern, or automated score should be the sole basis for a grade with consequences, a placement, a disability decision, or a behavior plan. Read the full decision boundary in Keep human decisions human.

Openness & limits

Inspect the code, the process, and what is still unfinished

A tool that sits between teachers and students should be plain about how it is licensed, how it is built, and where its evidence stops.

Open source

AlloFlow is licensed under GNU AGPL v3 with no license fee. Districts can inspect the code, host their own copy, and change it. A tool that sits between teachers and students should be open to the people it affects.

How AlloFlow is built

AlloFlow is built the same way it asks teachers to work. A practicing school psychologist sets the instructional and clinical direction, uses AI coding tools to draft code and documentation, and reviews and tests changes against automated checks before anything ships. AI accelerates the labor; a person stays accountable for what reaches a classroom.

Accessibility as ongoing work

We review AlloFlow against WCAG 2.2 A and AA and publish our accessibility conformance report, including what has not been tested. It is a working review, not a certification. Real assistive-technology testing with screen readers is still needed.
