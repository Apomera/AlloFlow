# Teacher Interview Notes

This is a completely synthetic research case. It does not describe a real student.

Interview date: March 13, 2026  
Informant: Third-grade classroom teacher

Jordan's teacher described Jordan as engaged, cooperative, and willing to attempt challenging work. Jordan contributes accurate ideas during class discussions and often explains multistep mathematics solutions clearly.

The teacher reported that Jordan reads familiar words more accurately than unfamiliar words. During oral reading, Jordan reads slowly, pauses frequently to decode, and sometimes substitutes a visually similar word. Jordan generally understands grade-level content when it is presented orally. When Jordan must read the same type of material independently, slow and inaccurate word reading reduces how much text Jordan completes and sometimes interferes with comprehension.

Jordan writes organized sentences and short paragraphs when spelling support is available. The teacher did not identify classroom concerns involving disruptive behavior, sustained attention during mathematics or discussion, peer relationships, or attendance.

Current classroom supports include previewing important vocabulary, providing additional time for independent reading, and allowing access to audio-supported grade-level content when the instructional goal is knowledge rather than decoding. The teacher recommended continuing intensive, explicit instruction in decoding and oral-reading fluency and monitoring progress frequently.
