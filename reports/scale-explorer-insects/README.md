# Honeybee, ladybird and hair shaft studies

Dedicated procedural models replace the earlier primitive forms. Each specimen has five selectable surfaces, cited notes and close camera views.

- Honeybee: shaped compound eyes, jointed antennae, a segmented abdomen, veined study wings and branched hairs.
- Ladybird: polished wing covers, articulated legs and membranous hindwings that deploy for inspection.
- Hair: overlapping cuticle scales, cortical fibres and pigment, plus an illustrative medulla. Inspecting inner layers reveals a prebuilt section.

The rulers retain the stated 13 mm and 6 mm insect body lengths and 70 micrometre shaft diameter. Appendages and the cropped shaft length stay outside those measurements. The figures are composed anatomical illustrations; colour, small structures and the representative hair layers are interpreted.
