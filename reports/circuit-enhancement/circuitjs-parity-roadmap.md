# CircuitJS coverage roadmap

Updated 10 September 2026. This compares circuit-simulation coverage, not visual similarity. Circuit Bench retains its own interface, projected 3D views, and learning workflows.

| Area | Circuit Bench now | Remaining work toward CircuitJS coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Network topology | Connected workspace: arbitrary connections among 8 nodes and 16 resistors, capacitors, inductors, generic diodes, independent/controlled ideal sources, finite-gain op-amps, wires, and switches; bridges and shared feeders | Larger networks, free placement/routing, reusable subcircuits, richer editing |
| Time simulation | Connected-network RLC startup/energy release, sine/triangle/finite-edge pulse drive, and scheduled switches with continuous stored-energy states; adaptive sampling and event diagnostics; existing passive and settled-AC branch experiments | Additional waveforms/integration methods, stronger conditioning and larger/stiffer networks |
| Analog devices | General-network silicon/Schottky junctions; four controlled-source families; finite-gain op-amps with output limits and operating-point diagnostics; existing LED and fixed-wiring NPN/sensor experiments | Op-amp dynamics and physical supply/output behavior; comparators and general BJT/FETs; Zener and diode charge/storage effects |
| 3D presentation | Projected component packages, two perspectives, adjustable tilt, zoom/pan/reset, routed rounded wires, sensing links, voltage colors, live amplifier state, and camera-aware label clearance | Full camera orbit and mesh rendering, richer lighting/materials, optional energy/field layers, electrical breadboard connectivity |
| Digital and mixed signal | Interactive reference material; no general digital network engine | Gates, clocks, latches, flip-flops, counters, timers, analog/digital thresholds and delays |
| Sources and instruments | DC and periodic voltage/current generators, signed probes, synchronized scope with matching-scale source comparison, before/after switch-event timeline, amplifier output-limit/error readouts, stored energy, reconstructible waveform CSV, input sweeps and active investigation reports | More waveforms, arbitrary multiple traces and triggering, spectrum tools |
| Advanced parts and interchange | Native saved workspaces; active investigation JSON; component/data exports | Coupled inductors/transformers, transmission lines and further device families; CircuitJS-compatible circuit exchange |

CircuitJS scope was checked against its [example catalog](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/e-index.html), [usage documentation](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/doc/overview.html), and [solver architecture](https://github.com/pfalstad/circuitjs1/blob/master/INTERNALS.md). Full parity also requires compatibility testing and numerical comparisons across a representative circuit suite; it cannot be established from the number of UI controls or a component list.

## Recommended implementation sequence

1. **Connected DC foundation — implemented in pass 22.** A shared node solver now supports bridges, shared loads, and multiple sources. It distinguishes unknown readings from zero, preserves internal differential readings in floating circuits, and rejects inconsistent constraints.
2. **General-network dynamics — linear startup, periodic drive, and timed switching implemented in passes 23–25.** Capacitor/inductor states, configurable initial energy, adaptive backward Euler integration, waveform-aware time steps, and synchronized scope/board/probes are available. Voltage/current sources support DC, sine, triangle, and finite-edge pulses with amplitude, frequency, phase, and pulse timing controls. Analytical startup, filter, ramp, and pulse checks cover the new drive modes. Switches support eight explicit timed actions each, simultaneous topology changes, carried capacitor voltage/inductor current, and before/after event snapshots. Charge/hold, switched discharge, and resistor flyback investigations include analytical and conservation checks. Next: more waveforms/integration methods and stronger numerical conditioning.
3. **General analog devices — diodes, controlled sources, and first op-amp stage implemented in passes 26–28.** Bounded nonlinear iteration now solves generic silicon/Schottky junctions in DC and time response, including half-wave/full-wave rectification, smoothing, and diode flyback. All four ideal controlled-source families now support voltage/current sensing, signed gains, and feedback in DC and time response. Finite-gain op-amps now clamp their output to configured limits, support feedback/filter experiments, and diagnose multiple operating points. Next add op-amp dynamics and comparator behavior, followed by general BJT/FET models and Zener/diode dynamic effects. Preserve numerical reliability and explicit model limits.
4. **Digital and mixed signal.** Introduce logic state, propagation timing, sequential devices, and analog thresholds using the shared network representation.
5. **Broader instruments and editing.** Expand traces, source waveforms, free placement/routing, subcircuits, and circuit-file interchange as the engine supports them.
6. **Extended catalog and parity audit.** Add remaining device families, compare a documented example set, and test stability, performance, keyboard use, and mobile layouts.

An electrically connected breadboard would be an additional teaching interface on the shared solver. It is not a prerequisite for matching CircuitJS’s simulation coverage.

Pass 25 adds scheduled ideal switching and event comparisons to the general linear network engine, following pass 24’s source waveforms. Pulse edges have finite duration; phase is applied at t = 0. DC equilibrium uses each source’s configured DC level alone, not its waveform average or RMS. Editing initial switch state, component values, or a schedule restarts the run. During a run, scheduled events preserve capacitor voltage and inductor current. Simultaneous changes are solved as one topology; incompatible events stop the calculation without a partial trace. Each switch allows up to eight actions from 1 µs to 60 s, with two snapshots at each event time. There is no periodic switch clock, contact bounce, arc, or impulse model. Backward Euler introduces numerical damping, and extreme settings or excessive visible cycles may hit a tolerance or step limit. Pass 26 adds memoryless exponential silicon/Schottky diode junctions. It does not include diode series resistance, reverse breakdown, junction capacitance, reverse recovery, or temperature variation. External resistance should be included where the circuit needs current limiting. This is not complete transient, nonlinear-device, or digital parity. The board remains a connection map: crossing lines join only when they share a named node.


## Further visual improvements, in useful order

1. **Routing foundation implemented in pass 27.** Rounded connections avoid unrelated packages, labels, and node controls; selected routes stay visible at crossings. All bundled examples routed without fallback in browser checks. Next improve congestion handling, user-controlled waypoints, and port placement; current paths are routed in display coordinates. The named-node netlist remains authoritative.
2. **Projected camera controls implemented in pass 27.** Two perspectives, adjustable tilt, zoom, mouse/touch/keyboard pan, reset, and persisted camera settings retain an accessible flat view and component/probe buttons. Full orbit is still future work; it should update depth sorting, occlusion, and hit testing together with a dedicated 3D layer.
3. **A dedicated 3D rendering layer.** Real meshes, soft contact shadows, consistent lighting, and material roughness would improve depth and realism beyond the current projected SVG geometry. Keep electrical state separate from rendering and scale quality for less powerful devices.
4. **Optional explanatory layers.** Show charge/current direction, capacitor energy, and inductor energy/field cues tied to the solved time cursor. Use explicit units and stable color ranges, support reduced motion, and distinguish illustrative fields from computed electromagnetic fields.
5. **Electrically modeled breadboard mode.** Map physical holes, shared strips, power rails, and inserted leads to the same solver nodes. A decorative board surface alone does not provide breadboard simulation.

## Next parity milestones

- **Op-amp dynamics, comparators, then general transistor families.** Pass 28 adds finite gain and hard output limits with clipping, follower, inverting, and active-filter lessons. Next add finite bandwidth, slew rate, saturation recovery, output-current and physical-supply behavior, then comparator timing/hysteresis and general BJT/FET devices. The present op-amp model is memoryless, internally powered, and limited to four amplifiers per network.
- **Broader semiconductor physics.** Zener breakdown, diode capacitance/recovery and series resistance, configurable temperature, and more detailed device parameters require additional state and convergence work. Current generic diode presets are teaching models rather than named commercial device models.
- **Digital/mixed-signal engine.** Gates, clock sources, hysteresis, propagation delay, latches/flip-flops, counters, and timers need a timing/event model linked to analog thresholds.
- **Larger circuits and editing.** Grow beyond 16 parts/8 named nodes, with placement/routing, reusable subcircuits, copy/paste, and validated circuit-file exchange. Do not label CSV measurements as CircuitJS netlist compatibility.
- **Analyses and instruments.** General AC small-signal sweeps around the operating point, more independent scope traces, triggers/cursors, spectral measurements, and additional integration choices. Existing fixed-topology passive AC lessons do not supply general-network AC analysis.
- **Magnetic and distributed components.** Coupled inductors/transformers and transmission lines need dedicated models and reference benchmarks.

The CircuitJS [example catalog](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/e-index.html) and [solver internals](https://github.com/pfalstad/circuitjs1/blob/master/INTERNALS.md) remain the reference for this staged comparison. Completion should be measured using documented circuit/analysis benchmarks, not merely matching menu entries.

Pass 27 adds ideal VCVS, VCCS, CCCS, and CCVS models with signed control readings in the scope/CSV and four guided investigations. Voltage sensing draws no current; current sensing refers to a voltage-source branch, including a 0 V series sensor. Ideal controlled outputs supply their own energy and omit rails, limits, and bandwidth. Camera and routing features improve the existing projected SVG board; they do not constitute full 3D orbit, physical breadboard connectivity, or complete CircuitJS parity. Validation is recorded in the [enhancement report](README.md#twenty-seventh-pass-controlled-sources-camera-navigation-and-routed-connections).

Pass 28 adds finite-gain, output-limited op-amps and live operating-point cards in the scope and inspector. Region checking rejects distinct operating points instead of selecting a latch state. Output limits are settings relative to output reference B; no physical supply terminals or internal dynamics are modeled. The [pass report](README.md#twenty-eighth-pass-finite-gain-op-amps-and-visible-output-limiting) records analytical, regression, visual, and accessibility checks.
