## Confirmed receiver gap

An in-flight RTC decode can restore and reopen a resource after the teacher removes it. In `AlloFlowANTI.txt`, the `res-remove` branch at lines 25600–25607 filters both resource lists but leaves active collection/decode records unchanged. The decode branch checks only chunk-store identity at line 25620 before appending the decoded resource to both lists and opening it at lines 25621–25633.

This interleaving is supported by real callsites: the mailbox poll awaits its own payloads at line 37060, while RTC invokes the same async receiver without awaiting at line 37181. Both share the same store. An RTC copy may already be decoding while mailbox replay skips its pending RID and processes a later removal. Completing the RTC decode then re-adds the removed item. The parent's baseline executes that ordering through exact production helpers/receiver with deferred decode promises and the real student-safe projection.

The random transfer IDs at lines 24992 and 25160 do not identify a resource before decode. Therefore a removal cannot simply locate a matching RID. Resetting the whole store would also invalidate unrelated transfers; permanently blacklisting resource IDs would block deliberate later sharing of the same resource.

The report-local candidate implements transfer-local invalidation: capture the existing pending record before awaiting decode; mark removal IDs on records that already existed when `res-remove` arrived; after decode, require the same pending record identity and suppress a decoded resource whose ID was removed during that record's lifetime. Retire a suppressed transfer through the real finish helper so its replay cannot revive it within the existing bounded applied cache. New RIDs start without old removal markers; an unrelated pending decoded ID remains valid within the explicit marker bound described below. Existing partial records carry their removal markers forward when promoted to pending. Production integration remains pending.

Any failure/retry implementation must preserve transfer-local invalidation through retries of the same old RID. A decode failure after removal must not discard the marker and permit old mailbox replay to restore the resource. A deliberate new-RID re-share must remain allowed. The coordinator can choose the smallest compatible bookkeeping representation without changing the network protocol.

## Existing safeguards

- The real receiver calls `_alloFinishResChunk(store, rid, true)` only after safe decoding/application at line 25634. Decode, JSON and student-projection failures call the same helper with `false` at line 25639. Cursor rewind retries failures until their per-RID count reaches three at line 25641.
- Collection refuses duplicates whose RID is applied, currently pending, or has three recorded failures (lines 1851–1852). Manual retry rotates the store, keeps successful applied RIDs and clears failed/partial records (lines 24705–24709).
- Session cleanup nulls the store (lines 24700–24704); joining creates a fresh store at line 36903. Pending success and failure callbacks compare store identity at lines 25620 and 25638, so earlier-session/manual-retry completion cannot apply into the replacement store.
- Incomplete `parts` records are capped at 16 (line 1855), per-packet data at 90 KiB and 256 parts (lines 1846–1848), one buffered transfer at 12 MiB and all incomplete buffers at 16 MiB (line 1862). Five-minute TTL pruning covers parts, pending and failures (lines 1811–1816), and active sessions prune every 30 seconds (line 24702). Successful applied RIDs are capped at 256 (line 1817).

Two additional source findings deserve care during the narrow fix. `pending` and `failures` have no independent count bound before TTL expiration; only incomplete `parts` and successful `applied` have count caps. Also, pruning an old pending record does not invalidate its still-awaiting callback: that callback can pass the store-identity guard, apply late, or finish a newer pending record with the same RID. Checking captured pending-record identity after await addresses the second gap without a protocol change. No stress test or five-minute browser failure was run, and no broad cache redesign is proposed here.
