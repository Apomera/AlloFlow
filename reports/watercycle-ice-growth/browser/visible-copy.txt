Inside-cloud lens: faint cyan points are teaching tracers for invisible water vapor. Rising air cools toward saturation at the glowing cloud base; vapor condenses on tiny nuclei into visible droplets and releases latent heat. Deposition: vapor adds directly to ice. Watch the six-armed crystals grow as hollow vapor tracers approach. These tracers represent invisible gas. Nearby droplets may stay liquid below 0°C (supercooled). Crystal shapes and timing are illustrative. This transparent cutaway reveals droplets and ice inside the shaded cloud volume. Its level underside marks the modeled saturation base. Updraft strength controls how long particles remain aloft. Particles are enlarged for teaching and are not to scale.
