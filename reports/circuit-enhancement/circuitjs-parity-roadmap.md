# CircuitJS coverage roadmap

Updated 12 September 2026. This compares circuit-simulation coverage, not visual similarity. Circuit Bench retains its own interface, orbitable mesh and projected/flat board views, and learning workflows.

CircuitJS scope was checked against its [example catalog](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/e-index.html), [usage documentation](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/doc/overview.html), and [solver architecture](https://github.com/pfalstad/circuitjs1/blob/master/INTERNALS.md). Full parity also requires compatibility testing and numerical comparisons across a representative circuit suite; it cannot be established from the number of UI controls or a component list.

## Current parity assessment

Circuit Bench is a capable small analog teaching workbench, with a shared numerical engine and extensive 3D/instrument workflows. It remains materially short of full CircuitJS coverage: general NPN/PNP transistor models are now available, but FET, digital, magnetic, and distributed-component families remain absent, and the editor remains limited to 16 parts and 8 named nodes. No defensible overall completion percentage exists without a weighted feature inventory and a shared reference-circuit benchmark. The 45 investigations are teaching examples, not a parity score.

The highest-impact next coverage stages are: (1) n/p-channel MOSFET models with independent biasing/switching benchmarks, followed by richer semiconductor dynamics; (2) digital gates, stateful logic, timers, thresholds and timing; (3) larger circuits, direct placement/routing, subcircuits and validated file exchange; (4) coupled inductors/transformers, transmission lines and remaining device families. Numerical conditioning and independent comparisons should accompany each stage. Optional physical breadboards and computed 3D fields are separate extensions.

## Recommended implementation sequence

1. **Connected DC foundation — implemented in pass 22.** A shared node solver now supports bridges, shared loads, and multiple sources. It distinguishes unknown readings from zero, preserves internal differential readings in floating circuits, and rejects inconsistent constraints.
2. **General-network dynamics — linear startup, periodic drive, and timed switching in passes 23–25; optional trapezoidal RLC integration in pass 45.** Capacitor/inductor states, configurable initial energy, adaptive backward Euler/trapezoidal RLC integration, waveform-aware time steps, and synchronized scope/board/probes are available. Voltage/current sources support DC, sine, triangle, and finite-edge pulses with amplitude, frequency, phase, and pulse timing controls. Analytical startup, filter, ramp, and pulse checks cover the new drive modes. Switches support eight explicit timed actions each, simultaneous topology changes, carried capacitor voltage/inductor current, and before/after event snapshots. Charge/hold, switched discharge, and resistor flyback investigations include analytical and conservation checks. Trapezoidal reduces numerical damping in smooth RLC responses; backward Euler remains the default, handles restart intervals, and always advances timed op-amps. Next: more waveforms and stronger numerical conditioning.
3. **General analog devices — diodes, controlled sources, and static/timed op-amp stages implemented in passes 26–29, with Zener breakdown in pass 37, diode series resistance in pass 38, and general NPN/PNP transistors in pass 46.** Bounded nonlinear iteration now solves generic silicon/Schottky junctions in DC and time response, including half-wave/full-wave rectification, smoothing, and diode flyback. All four ideal controlled-source families now support voltage/current sensing, signed gains, and feedback in DC and time response. Finite-gain op-amps now clamp their output to configured limits, support feedback/filter experiments, and diagnose multiple operating points. Optional one-pole bandwidth and symmetric slew limits now add startup, gain-bandwidth, and distortion lessons; amplifier output is carried continuously across switch events. Pass 37 adds a configurable Zener breakdown branch, regulation and clipping investigations, and a visual I–V explorer. Pass 38 adds internal series resistance, a dimensional equivalent-model inspector, terminal/junction curve comparison, and three current-driven investigations. Pass 46 adds memoryless Ebers–Moll NPN/PNP models, three-terminal nonlinear stamping, independent collector/base/emitter current and total-power readings, three-lead board geometry, and switching/follower/amplifier/mirror investigations. Next add n/p-channel MOSFETs, physical amplifier behavior and comparator timing/hysteresis, then junction charge/storage and temperature effects. Preserve numerical reliability and explicit model limits.
4. **Digital and mixed signal.** Introduce logic state, propagation timing, sequential devices, and analog thresholds using the shared network representation.
5. **Broader instruments and editing — source-referenced harmonic exploration implemented in pass 39.** The scope now supports H1–H12 voltage/current harmonics, linear/log amplitude views, DC/RMS, explicitly limited-order THD, summed/isolated reconstruction, and SI coefficients. Next expand arbitrary-frequency spectra, window functions, independent traces, source waveforms, free placement/routing, subcircuits, and circuit-file interchange as the engine supports them.
6. **Extended catalog and parity audit.** Add remaining device families, compare a documented example set, and test stability, performance, keyboard use, and mobile layouts.

An electrically connected breadboard would be an additional teaching interface on the shared solver. It is not a prerequisite for matching CircuitJS’s simulation coverage.

Pass 25 adds scheduled ideal switching and event comparisons to the general linear network engine, following pass 24’s source waveforms. Pulse edges have finite duration; phase is applied at t = 0. DC equilibrium uses each source’s configured DC level alone, not its waveform average or RMS. Editing initial switch state, component values, or a schedule restarts the run. During a run, scheduled events preserve capacitor voltage and inductor current. Simultaneous changes are solved as one topology; incompatible events stop the calculation without a partial trace. Each switch allows up to eight actions from 1 µs to 60 s, with two snapshots at each event time. There is no periodic switch clock, contact bounce, arc, or impulse model. Backward Euler introduces numerical damping, and extreme settings or excessive visible cycles may hit a tolerance or step limit. Pass 26 adds memoryless exponential silicon/Schottky diode junctions. Those presets do not include diode series resistance, reverse breakdown, junction capacitance, reverse recovery, or temperature variation. Pass 37 adds a separate generic Zener preset with an adjustable sharp breakdown knee and constant reverse slope resistance. External resistance should be included where the circuit needs current limiting. This is not complete transient, nonlinear-device, or digital parity. The board remains a connection map: crossing lines join only when they share a named node.


## Further visual improvements, in useful order

1. **Routing foundation implemented in pass 27.** Rounded connections avoid unrelated packages, labels, and node controls; selected routes stay visible at crossings. All bundled examples routed without fallback in browser checks. Next improve congestion handling, user-controlled waypoints, and port placement; projected-view paths are routed in display coordinates. Pass 40 maps the flat routing plan into fixed world-space paths for orbit mode. The named-node netlist remains authoritative.
2. **Projected camera controls implemented in pass 27.** Two perspectives, adjustable tilt, zoom, mouse/touch/keyboard pan, reset, and persisted camera settings retain an accessible flat view and component/probe buttons. Pass 40 adds a separate real 3D orbit mode with depth-tested meshes, picking, saved camera state, and aspect-aware fitting. The projected and flat cameras remain available.
3. **Dedicated mesh rendering implemented in pass 40.** Orbit mode adds real component/lead/board geometry, lighting, roughness/metalness, shadows, ray-based picking, optional gestures, accessible camera controls, and shared solver overlays. It renders on demand, caps pixel ratio, offers lightweight graphics, and disposes its resources on exit. Next refine device geometry, placement/routing tools, and quality scaling across hardware while keeping electrical state separate from rendering.
4. **Stored-energy layer implemented in pass 33.** Capacitor/inductor gauges share a whole-run joule scale, with signed power, energy changes, peak-sample navigation, and quadratic-energy explanations. The meters follow the solved cursor and remain static. Pass 41 adds optional current-direction trails on the real 3D leads, manual stepping, terminal badges, and signed current/power explanations with reduced-motion support. The markers describe a fixed solved snapshot and do not encode particle speed or advance time. Further layers could add illustrative field geometry; computed electromagnetic fields require a different model.
5. **Electrically modeled breadboard mode.** Map physical holes, shared strips, power rails, and inserted leads to the same solver nodes. A decorative board surface alone does not provide breadboard simulation.

## Next parity milestones

- **MOSFETs, physical amplifier behavior, and comparators.** General NPN/PNP models are implemented in pass 46. Next implement independently benchmarked n/p-channel MOSFET models with clear region and body-diode limits. Passes 28–29 add finite gain, hard output limits, optional one-pole bandwidth and slew rate, and feedback/startup/distortion lessons. Next add output impedance/current limits, physical supplies, input common-mode/offset behavior, additional poles and internal overload-storage recovery, then comparator timing/hysteresis. The present op-amp remains internally powered and limited to four amplifiers per network. Timing can be disabled for the earlier memoryless behavior; a one-pole time response does not provide general small-signal or stability analysis.
- **Broader semiconductor physics.** Pass 37 implements a continuous piecewise Zener reverse-breakdown branch; pass 38 adds optional internal series resistance in both current directions. Pass 46 adds memoryless bipolar transport in four bias regions. Next add soft knees, diode and transistor capacitance/recovery, configurable temperature, Early effect/breakdown, and more detailed device parameters with independent reference benchmarks and appropriate convergence/state handling. Current generic diode presets are teaching models rather than named commercial device models.
- **Digital/mixed-signal engine.** Gates, clock sources, hysteresis, propagation delay, latches/flip-flops, counters, and timers need a timing/event model linked to analog thresholds.
- **Larger circuits and editing.** Grow beyond 16 parts/8 named nodes, with placement/routing, reusable subcircuits, copy/paste, and validated circuit-file exchange. Do not label CSV measurements as CircuitJS netlist compatibility.
- **Analyses and instruments.** General AC small-signal sweeps around the operating point, more independent scope traces, additional independent trace cursors, live capture-style triggers and hysteresis, arbitrary-frequency spectra/window functions/noise analysis beyond the pass-39 source-referenced harmonics, and further solver conditioning. Pass 45 adds optional trapezoidal RLC integration; timed op-amps retain backward Euler. Existing fixed-topology passive AC lessons do not supply general-network AC analysis.
- **Magnetic and distributed components.** Coupled inductors/transformers and transmission lines need dedicated models and reference benchmarks.

The CircuitJS [example catalog](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/e-index.html) and [solver internals](https://github.com/pfalstad/circuitjs1/blob/master/INTERNALS.md) remain the reference for this staged comparison. Completion should be measured using documented circuit/analysis benchmarks, not merely matching menu entries.

Pass 27 adds ideal VCVS, VCCS, CCCS, and CCVS models with signed control readings in the scope/CSV and four guided investigations. Voltage sensing draws no current; current sensing refers to a voltage-source branch, including a 0 V series sensor. Ideal controlled outputs supply their own energy and omit rails, limits, and bandwidth. Camera and routing features improve the existing projected SVG board; they do not constitute full 3D orbit, physical breadboard connectivity, or complete CircuitJS parity. Validation is recorded in the [enhancement report](README.md#twenty-seventh-pass-controlled-sources-camera-navigation-and-routed-connections).

Pass 28 adds finite-gain, output-limited op-amps and live operating-point cards in the scope and inspector. Region checking rejects distinct operating points instead of selecting a latch state. Output limits are settings relative to output reference B; no physical supply terminals are modeled. This pass introduced the memoryless mode, which remains available with timing disabled. The [pass report](README.md#twenty-eighth-pass-finite-gain-op-amps-and-visible-output-limiting) records analytical, regression, visual, and accessibility checks.


Pass 29 adds optional one-pole bandwidth, symmetric slew limits, and a configurable starting output to the connected op-amp. Four guided investigations distinguish bandwidth loss/phase lag, gain-bandwidth tradeoffs, slew distortion, and startup settling. Time response carries amplifier output across switch events; DC equilibrium ignores timing and initial output. Scope/board readouts and CSV expose local slope, integration slope, and active timing state. The [pass report](README.md#twenty-ninth-pass-op-amp-bandwidth-slew-rate-and-continuous-timing-state) records the model equations, analytical checks, 319-test regression, and 30-example browser coverage. Additional poles, physical input/output/supply behavior, overload storage, and comparator timing remain separate stages.

Pass 30 adds a scope measurement window with start/end cursors, exact interval entry, whole-run/last-source-cycle presets, time-weighted statistics, and independent-sine-source gain/phase comparison. A fitted-sine overlay and residual describe the chosen portion of the simulated trace. These measurements do not establish steady state or provide general AC sweeps, stability margins, FFT/THD, or automatic triggering. The [pass report](README.md#thirtieth-pass-measurement-windows-and-sine-comparison) documents the algorithms, event-boundary conventions, and verification.

Pass 31 adds bounded 1–64× time zoom, S–E interval focus, an overview/pan control, keyboard navigation, and optional signal-range voltage scaling for small amplifier responses. A level-crossing finder searches selected voltage/current traces in the whole calculated run, with rising/falling filtering, sample-aware navigation, exact switch-side handling, held-level intervals, same-direction spacing, and CSV export. This is recorded-trace search, not live capture, trigger re-arming, hysteresis, or additional simulator time resolution. The [pass report](README.md#thirty-first-pass-time-zoom-and-level-crossing-navigation) records implementation and validation.


Pass 32 improves the projected board materials, direct package selection, camera focus, grid/emphasis controls, and probe feedback. A live instrument dock joins the board to its existing scope and calculated snapshots, including exact before/after switch states. These additions improve presentation and workflow; they do not add device coverage or a new rendering engine. See the [validation and screenshots](README.md#thirty-second-pass-dimensional-board-materials-and-live-instruments).


Pass 33 adds a stored-energy board overlay and explorer using the existing calculated capacitor/inductor energy and power. A common joule scale supports meaningful visual comparisons; peak navigation retains exact switch sides. It adds no new device physics or numerical integration method. See the [validation and screenshots](README.md#thirty-third-pass-stored-energy-gauges-and-learning-explorer).


Pass 34 adds per-node connection and current-balance inspection, separate voltage-sensing links, directional branch highlighting, node focusing, and explicit node-to-reference probing. It improves topology understanding and board workflow using the existing solver snapshots. This improves the 3D learning view without implementing computed electromagnetic fields, device geometry, new component models, or full orbit. Pass 37 adds an adjustable generic Zener junction in DC and time response, three regulation/clipping investigations, a linear I–V curve with forward/reverse/breakdown-detail views and shared held/cursor points, explicit bias states, a distinctive projected package, and appended CSV model metadata. The knee is the onset of a continuous piecewise-linear reverse branch, not a datasheet voltage rated at a test current. Soft knees, charge/storage, temperature, noise, ratings, and damage remain outside this model. This is another analog coverage step; it does not provide full CircuitJS parity. See the [validation and screenshots](README.md#thirty-seventh-pass-zener-regulation-clipping-and-junction-curves).


Pass 38 adds optional internal diode resistance with milliohm precision, an implicit terminal/junction solve, chain-rule terminal conductance, and forward iteration limiting based on the internal junction voltage. The curve compares the complete diode with the same junction at Rs = 0. A dimensional equivalent-model view exposes signed voltage contributions and absorbed-power shares. The 36-example catalog now includes three current-driven resistance investigations. The junction remains memoryless and the model does not calculate temperature, recovery, or damage. See the [validation and screenshots](README.md#thirty-eighth-pass-internal-diode-resistance-and-voltagepower-exploration).


Pass 39 adds an optional source-referenced harmonic explorer with selectable H1–H12 bars, linear/log amplitude views, DC and AC RMS, explicitly limited-order THD, and whole/single-harmonic reconstruction. Three guided investigations connect symmetry, clipping, and filtering to spectral content, bringing the connected catalog to 39. The analysis directly integrates existing piecewise-linear samples over whole source cycles, with worst-gap resolution guards and explicit unavailable readings. It does not add arbitrary-frequency FFT spectra, window functions, noise models, general AC analysis, or new circuit physics. See the [validation and screenshots](README.md#thirty-ninth-pass-harmonic-exploration-and-waveform-reconstruction).

The layer preserves the existing circuit solver, editor, scope, and held references; illustrative geometry does not add physical breadboard connectivity, fields, or new device behavior. See the [validation and screenshots](README.md#fortieth-pass-orbitable-3d-board-and-shared-simulation-state).

Pass 45 adds optional adaptive trapezoidal integration for capacitor/inductor companions, backward Euler restarts after startup/source corners/switching or unknown history, method-aware held references, and per-sample CSV provenance. A new ideal-LC investigation distinguishes numerical damping from resistor losses, bringing the catalog to 40 examples. Timed op-amps retain their backward Euler dynamics and mixed runs use a conservative first-order error estimate. In the 100 mH/100 µF, 5 V, 100 ms benchmark, trapezoidal retains 99.99855% of initial energy with 0.02328 V maximum voltage error; backward Euler retains 87.92434% with 0.30982 V maximum error. These are one circuit’s results, not a global accuracy guarantee. All 39 earlier examples preserve their exact default trajectory and also solve with the new preference. This adds numerical-method coverage, not transistor/digital coverage or CircuitJS file compatibility. See the [implementation and validation](README.md#forty-fifth-pass-trapezoidal-rlc-integration-and-a-candid-parity-audit).


Pass 46 adds general NPN/PNP transistors using independently written, memoryless Ebers–Moll equations in DC and time response. Collector, base, and emitter currents each enter the node balance; total absorbed power includes base drive. Both polarities have three-lead flat/projected/orbit geometry, correctly signed current trails, junction probe actions, operating-region explanations, and explicit CSV metadata. Five switching, follower, amplifier, and mirror investigations bring the catalog to 45. All 40 prior default trajectories are unchanged, and all 45 solve with both RLC integration preferences. Charge storage, junction capacitance, Early effect, breakdown, temperature, device ratings, and unique nonlinear-feedback equilibria are outside this teaching model. MOSFETs, digital/timers, larger editing/subcircuits/interchange, and magnetic/distributed devices remain major parity stages. See the [implementation and validation](README.md#forty-sixth-pass-general-npn-and-pnp-transistors).
