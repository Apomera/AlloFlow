# Tree Life Lab improvements

Implemented from the September 4 engagement review.

- The default Grow view now offers a short drought discovery: predict, compare two copies of the same starting tree over three years, interpret evidence, and try recovery. Only water differs between the two trials. Saved comparisons survive subsequent exploration.

- Intervention panels and the small scene badge explicitly label earlier changes. Their wording no longer conflicts with the live conditions.
- Very small maintenance costs remain visibly nonzero, including in the seasonal ledger. Limiting-factor explanations now acknowledge interacting inputs instead of promising that every other input has no effect.

The second pass connects root investment and shade tolerance to the annual biological equations. This remains a qualitative teaching model; the new coefficients are explicit teaching assumptions rather than measurements.

- Root mass relative to leaf area now affects a bounded water-access index. Additional roots help an undersupplied canopy, with diminishing returns and an ongoing carbon cost. Roots cannot extract water from completely dry soil. The former minimum root-mass floor was removed because it could create roots without surplus carbon.
- Shade tolerance improves low-light capture and carries a small maximum-capacity cost. Photosynthesis remains nondecreasing as light increases. Other species traits still matter, so the trait does not guarantee a winning species.
- A keyboard-accessible section beside the allocation controls explains the tradeoff. It shows current water access, root upkeep, and a one-year preview that responds to allocation changes without advancing the tree. The preview explicitly holds the current weather steady and includes changes in both roots and leaves.
- Live readouts, condition-change feedback, annual steps, controlled trials, response curves and the seasonal ledger all use the same physiology function. Wilting uses the same root-dependent water access. Earlier saved observations remain historical results.

### Model assumptions and provenance

For root carbon mass R and leaf area A, uptake capacity is 1.25R / (R + 0.025A), or zero without roots. The effective water-access index is the soil-water setting multiplied by capacity, clamped to 0–1. A root-to-leaf-area ratio of 0.1 kg C/m² gives capacity 1; unlimited roots approach 1.25. This index feeds both stomatal opening and the existing water multiplier. It is not a water volume, a new soil-moisture measurement, or a soil-water balance. Soil depth, root architecture, rainfall, hydraulic failure and depletion are not simulated.

The concept of uptake capacity relative to canopy demand follows the root-to-leaf relationships discussed by [Comas et al., Root traits contributing to plant productivity under drought](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2013.00442/full). That review emphasizes functional root area/length and morphology; using root carbon mass as a proxy here is a deliberate simplification, not a coefficient obtained from that study.

For shade tolerance s, the half-saturation parameter in the light response is 0.22(1 − 0.65s); maximum photosynthetic capacity is multiplied by 1 − 0.12s. This is a stylized low-light efficiency versus capacity tradeoff. Real shade tolerance involves multiple mechanisms: for example, the [USDA Forest Service account of American beech](https://www.srs.fs.usda.gov/pubs/misc/ag_654/volume_2/fagus/grandifolia.htm) describes low respiration and responsive stomata. That source does not validate this equation or its species coefficients. The model retains its existing species respiration rates and does not simulate acclimation.

Validation covers zero-water and zero-root boundaries, diminishing returns, scale consistency, delayed allocation effects, carbon upkeep, excessive-root tradeoffs, monotonic light response, isolated shade-trait comparisons, and agreement between live, annual, seasonal and trial calculations. The original 60-year growth and longer survival checks still pass. Browser checks cover the allocation preview and learning workflows. These checks establish internal consistency, not empirical biological accuracy or measured learning gains.

### Sixth pass: campaign 3D close-up

### Seventh pass: evidence, prediction, mortality and replay comparison

A probe of 48 worlds showed that no campaign tree ever died, so "trees lost", the lineage-record wording and the Keep reserves card carried no real consequence. One explicit scenario rule now adds that consequence: trees three years old or younger face an extra mortality draw when their patch's soil moisture falls below 35 percent that year, scaled by how dry the patch is and reduced by the species' drought tolerance. The draw is keyed on year and individual, so it is independent of the weather and of other trees, and the same code still replays identically. Older trees still die only through the existing food-reserve rule. The forecast warns about the risk in dry years and the disclosure states the assumption.

Each year's receipt now records every landing (patch, route, outcome and the condition that most reduced the odds: dry soil, shade, no space or chance alone) and every loss (patch, species, age and cause). A "Where it happened" list in the evidence reads those records patch by patch. On the habitat map, dead trees stay as bare grey snag glyphs behind the living crowns, patch counts include snags, and a badge on each patch marks this year's arrivals (+) and losses (−); patch labels for screen readers carry the same information.

Before living through a year, an optional predict-then-check step asks how many arrivals to expect and whether the grove will make more food than it uses. The evidence then reports Matched or Missed with a one-sentence reason drawn from the year's conditions. Predictions live beside the run, not inside the save format; rewinding drops the prediction for the undone year, and replaying or returning to setup clears them.

Completed runs are added to a small ledger (at most twelve, keyed by code, mode and choices). The ending card lists other completed runs of the same code and mode, showing the priority sequence, established descendants, patches, living trees and whether the goal was reached, with a note that identical weather makes the comparison fair. Setup shows how many runs of the current code are remembered. This supports the recommended learner check about fair replays without adding genetics, invasive species or a full forest view.

### Eighth pass: stored food, grouped evidence, discoveries and a journey chart

Each receipt now records the whole grove's stored food and reproductive savings across living trees, and the evidence card reports them with the change from the previous year. The Keep reserves card therefore leaves a visible trail even in years when nothing dies, which the seventh-pass ledger comparison had shown to be its weak point.

"Where it happened" groups patches whose landings had identical outcomes onto one line ("Sunny ridge, Open meadow, Stream bend: 1 failed each, dry soil lowered the odds."), so a dry year reads as one fact instead of six near-identical lines. Losses keep their own lines.

Three discoveries join the field journal: losing a young tree to dry soil (derived from the run), making a prediction that matched the evidence, and completing two runs of the same grove code. The last two are earned when a year or run is saved and, like the others, recognise observations rather than granting bonuses.

The field journal opens with a small line chart of living trees and established descendants by year. It follows the shared chart rules: one axis, two-pixel lines, eight-pixel markers with a surface ring, a legend plus end labels, hover titles on every point, and the existing timeline list as the text and table alternative. The green and amber pair was checked with the palette validator against the light card and the dark card surfaces; the second series is dashed with square markers so identity never depends on colour alone, and high contrast uses white and yellow. The chart is text tokens and marks only; no value wears a series colour.

### Ninth pass: forecast risk preview and reflection notes

The forecast now says what next year's weather means on the ground. Every patch whose prescribed soil moisture will fall below 35 percent is listed under "Dry next year", the number of young trees standing in those patches is counted, and the card reminds learners that new arrivals rarely take root in dry soil. On the map those patches get a dashed border and a small "dry next" chip, their labels say so for screen readers, and the selected-patch panel shows next year's moisture beside the light setting. The preview disappears once the run has ended. Nothing here changes the simulation; it reads the same environment function the engine uses.

The ending card gains a reflection field for the completed run, asking what the learner would try differently and why. The note is saved into that run's ledger entry, so later comparisons of the same grove code show it beside the numbers, and a fresh replay starts with an empty field. Journal discovery and timeline lists now show their bullets and numbers.
