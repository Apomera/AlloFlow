## Science and model review

The display reads the existing matter/energy and hydrologic-time descriptors. Water transport and latent heat are separate observations. Evaporation absorbs heat; condensation releases heat to surrounding air, consistent with USGS explanations of [evaporation](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evaporation-and-water-cycle) and [condensation](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/condensation-and-water-cycle). A descriptor with no required phase change does not claim that all energy transfer is zero. Arrow direction is qualitative; the diagram does not measure energy, water amount or physical travel time.

Root uptake and xylem transport keep water liquid; leaf evaporation returns vapor to the air. USGS distinguishes these steps in its [transpiration explanation](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evapotranspiration-and-water-cycle). Infiltration can retain water in shallow soil, while groundwater can later discharge into surface water. These distinctions follow USGS descriptions of [infiltration](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/infiltration-and-water-cycle) and [groundwater flow](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topic/water-science-school/science/groundwater-discharge-and-water-cycle).
