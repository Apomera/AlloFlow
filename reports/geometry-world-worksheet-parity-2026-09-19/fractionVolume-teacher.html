Build, observe, calculate, and explain.

Geometry World uses the familiar building and exploration of block-based 3D sandbox games to make geometric measurement visible. Count cubes in a layer, connect layers to multiplication, and compare strategies. Explaining and checking those calculations provides practice toward accurate, flexible calculation fluency.

What happens when dimensions aren’t whole numbers? Explore how half-blocks change volume calculations.

Lesson goals

  1. Compare a 4×3×2 prism to a 4×3×2.5 prism
  2. Calculate volume when one dimension is a fraction
  3. Understand that volume can be a non-whole number
  4. Estimate the volume of a partially filled container

Between Quiz

Guide: Between Quiz at world coordinates (12, 5, 3). Use this sheet alongside the lesson world. Observe the named models or guide before answering; sketch and label the model so your reasoning can be understood on paper.

Mentor teaching

The blue prism = 24. The gold = 48. What about a height halfway between (2.5)?

Question 1 · Step 1

Blue is 4×3×2 = 24. Gold is 4×3×4 = 48. Halfway height is?

  1. 3.5
  2. 2.5 (halfway between 2 and 4-1=3)
  3. 3

Authored answer: B. 2.5 (halfway between 2 and 4-1=3)

Question 2 · Step 2

Volume of 4 × 3 × 2.5?

  1. 24 cubic units
  2. 36 cubic units
  3. 30 cubic units

Authored answer: C. 30 cubic units

Question 3 · Step 3

Is 30 between 24 and 48?

  1. Yes! Fractional height gives in-between volume
  2. No
  3. Only sometimes

Authored answer: A. Yes! Fractional height gives in-between volume
