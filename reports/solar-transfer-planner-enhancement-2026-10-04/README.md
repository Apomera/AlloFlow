# Solar System transfer planner enhancement · 2026-10-04

The Transfers tab now connects route selection, burn inspection, the existing rendezvous experiment, and comparisons from Earth. All work remains uncommitted.

## What changed

- Two named, responsive eight-planet selectors show the resolved route. Readable route metrics expose departure/arrival/total Δv, coast time, transfer axis, signed launch phase, and approximate synodic spacing. Same-planet routes show zero burns/time and explicitly inapplicable alignment/window readings.
- Departure and arrival burn views show speeds before/after the impulse, signed speed change, and distance from the Sun. Both views share one speed ruler; switching views preserves the mounted flight and its local progress.
- Seven Earth-route buttons compare absolute departure/arrival budgets on one 0–20 km/s ruler, using patterned and solid segments. Selecting a route updates the two planet settings and moves keyboard focus to the planner heading.
- The exact table remains in an accessible disclosure with a named keyboard-scrollable region. Its pinned destination and two-line transit value remain readable together at 320px.
- Scoped styles provide 44px controls, readable light/dark surfaces, visible selected/focus states, and a canvas whose logical width matches its displayed content. Canvas labels use native 13px text and retain one AU distance scale.
- The transfer-specific Kepler solve now uses a bounded bracket. The previous Newton solve produced incorrect craft positions for extreme routes, including Mercury→Neptune at 6.3%/7.6% and Neptune→Mercury at 93.7%/93.8%. Other orbital models retain their existing solvers.
- Invalid/non-planet restored route IDs derive Earth/Mars defaults consistently without rewriting saves on render. Malformed debrief records, writing, snapshots and follow-up metadata derive safe display values; valid writing and raw saves remain intact until a deliberate action.

The only new saved setting is `orr_trburn` (`departure` or `arrival`). Existing flight, comparison, arrival inspection, history, journal and mission flows remain available.

## Model and limits

The tool uses ideal circular, coplanar orbits around the Sun. Burns are changes in heliocentric speed; arrival circularization is separate from a planetary capture maneuver. Budgets exclude launch from a surface, planetary escape/capture, plane changes and corrections. Synodic spacing uses the existing rounded orbital periods and is approximate, not a calendar of launch dates. NASA describes the tangent transfer geometry, target timing, inward braking and separate capture process in [Basics of Space Flight, Chapter 4](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter4-1/).
