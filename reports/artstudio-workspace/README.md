The September 28 editing pass adds **Rotate selection**, **Flip selection ↔**, and **Flip selection ↕** to the pixel region controls. Rotating a rectangle swaps its width and height; the result shifts inward at the canvas edge so every selected cell fits. Transformations keep exact colors, preserve artwork outside the source and painted destination, and commit one undo step. Empty destination cells retain existing background colors. The internal clipboard is unchanged, and an unchanged transformation does not create redundant history. Transform buttons are disabled during unfinished selections and drags.

Watercolor brush sampling now follows the narrowest part of the pressure/tilt footprint while retaining paint load per travelled distance. Flat and rigger brushes take the shortest turn across angle wraparound, preventing an unintended wide blot. Stationary stylus samples update pressure, tilt, and timing without adding paint. High-frequency input no longer receives the slower-speed paint load caused by the former four-millisecond floor. The dispatched endpoint is included after coalesced events, so devices that omit it from their bundled samples still finish the stroke. These changes retain single-gesture undo and the existing saved-paint format.

Pixel drawing now interpolates all cells between pointer samples, completes fast release endpoints, ignores unrelated pointers, and publishes live strokes for host autosave. Undo and redo operate on whole gestures, clearing, and resolution changes. Grids support 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, and 64 cells per side. Resolution changes resample the existing composition with nearest-neighbor mapping; Undo recovers the original resolution and pixels. PNG export excludes grid lines and the keyboard cursor.

Straight lines preview while dragging and commit on release. Keyboard users select both endpoints with Enter, and Escape or pointer cancellation discards the preview without changing saved work or undo history. Color picking reuses a painted cell and returns to the brush. Horizontal and vertical mirroring work with the brush, eraser, and lines. Custom HSL controls complement the palettes. Transparent sprite export writes one image pixel per grid cell; the standard PNG export remains available. History and active strokes are invalidated when a saved study is forked or the learner changes.

The latest brush pass adds 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8-cell square or round tips for painting and erasing. Brush coverage can be solid, 25%, 50%, or 75%. These ordered pixel patterns stay anchored to the artwork across overlapping strokes, preserving the other color between marks. Erasing always clears the full chosen footprint. Pixel selection now supports rectangular regions, moving, copying, cutting, pasting, deleting, and selecting all. Drag inside a selected region to move it, or nudge with Arrow keys (Shift moves four cells). Keyboard users set opposite corners with Enter. Moves are clamped to the grid and read from an unchanged source to preserve overlapping pixels. The view follows the selection's keyboard anchor when nudging zoomed artwork. Empty cells in a pasted region preserve destination colors. Drag previews stay out of autosave and exports; releasing commits one undo step, while cancellation leaves the artwork untouched. The clipboard is local to the current canvas and clears when the learner, study, or grid size changes. Copy/Cut/Paste support Ctrl or Cmd shortcuts, and the action buttons are tested through real clicks.

Watercolor now renders mobile and settled pigment as separate translucent absorption layers. Existing paint no longer changes when the next brush's pigment load changes. Wet washes darken slightly with moisture, paper valleys affect granulation and absorption, and drying boundaries gain concentration detail. Brush deposits have fuller bodies and paper-dependent edges, with dry brushes skipping paper valleys. Pigment saturation limits the entire color parcel, preventing repeatedly painted strokes from bleaching. Stroke sampling normalizes deposit by travel distance and handles lost pointer capture.

The deeper simulation pass replaces neighbor averaging with conservative transfers. Every pigment and RGB transfer is subtracted from its source and added to its destination, including fast and slow mobility pools. The previous implementation gained approximately 12% pigment during a 50-step wash without any new paint; the regression now checks that total density and each color channel stay constant. Flow carries pigment downhill, dry paint remains still, and saturated glazes retain excess pigment on the dry surface instead of clipping it away. Rewetting mobilizes deposited paint according to staining resistance. The new Wet paper control prepares a clear, evenly damp sheet, respects masking fluid, and supports undo. Brush edges respond to existing paper moisture, producing firmer dry-paper edges and softer marks on wet paper; dry-brush texture uses fine tooth rather than large missing patches.

Watercolor now shows a separate brush outline for hover, stylus drawing, and keyboard positioning. Its shape follows the same footprint calculation used for painting, including size, pressure, tilt, and brush type. It is a DOM overlay and never enters the PNG or saved paint state. Light pressure produces a fine tip while firm pressure broadens the stroke; zero-pressure pen release no longer falls back to a medium-pressure dab. The simulation also settles the last traces of moisture before stopping its animation, then saves that final dry state. This prevents tiny residual amounts of water and mobile pigment from remaining indefinitely after the animation goes idle.

Watercolor has an expanded workspace with a large paper area, a scrollable settings column, and its painting actions beside the canvas. The same canvas and simulation remain mounted when entering or exiting native fullscreen or the embedded-browser fallback. A finger-painting toggle beside the color picker stays synchronized with the detailed touch controls. On phones, the toolbar keeps its natural height and the workspace scrolls vertically. A pre-existing Undo/Redo button issue is also corrected: the controls' visible disabled state now agrees with click handling, so mouse and touch actions work as well as keyboard shortcuts.

Salt now responds to damp mobile pigment instead of erasing both wet and dry paint. Irregular crystal centers redistribute complete pigment/color/trait parcels to nearby rims while absorbing a little water. Settled glazes stay unchanged, dry gaps and masking fluid block transfers, and saturated destinations limit the whole parcel. Very wet puddles weaken the effect. Salt no longer drains the brush's water reservoir. This is an immediate texture approximation informed by [Winsor & Newton's salt and granulation demonstration](https://www.winsornewton.com/blogs/masterclass/using-watercolour-granulation-medium-with-salt), rather than a simulation of dissolving salt over time.

The canvas has a stable 1024 × 1024 backing store and exports clean PNGs at that resolution. Portable snapshots remain 512 × 512, and the existing 192 × 192 simulation/state format is preserved for saved work. This is a browser-oriented approximation: it does not implement a spectral pigment model or a full fluid solver. The separate-glaze approach is informed by [Curtis et al., Computer-Generated Watercolor](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/watercolor/).
